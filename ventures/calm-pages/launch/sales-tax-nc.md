# Sales tax: Peace by Page on Payhip, NC seller (launch step 20)

Researched 10/5/2026 from official sources. This is a plain-language summary, not legal or tax advice. For a firm answer, call NCDOR (1-877-252-3052) or ask a tax preparer.

## Short answer

1. **Does NC tax a printable PDF journal sold to an NC buyer?** Very likely yes. Since 2016, North Carolina taxes "certain digital property" at the normal sales tax rate (4.75% state plus county and transit, about 6.75% to 7.5%). That category covers digital books and "a magazine, a newspaper, a newsletter, a report, or another publication" delivered electronically. A downloadable journal or workbook fits "digital book" or "other publication". NC has no exemption for printables, and it doesn't matter that the buyer prints it.
2. **Does Payhip collect and pay it?** Yes. Payhip says it became a marketplace facilitator for US sales tax on **July 1, 2026**. It now calculates, collects and pays US state sales tax on your sales, at no charge to you.
3. **Must Tim register with NCDOR?** At your volume, very likely **no**, as long as every sale goes through Payhip. NCDOR's own FAQ says a marketplace seller who has a physical presence in NC and sells only through a marketplace facilitator that collects the tax does **not** have to register or file a return. (A possible exception: Payhip's help page says sellers may still need to file "zero dollar returns" in their home state. NC's FAQ says no for NC, so NC's rule wins here.)

## What Tim should do

- In Payhip, open **Settings > Tax** and choose whether tax is added on top of the price (the usual US choice) or included in it.
- On each product, under **Advanced options**, tick **Customize product tax category** and pick the closest match for a downloadable book or printable. If none fits, leave the default and ask Payhip support (contact@payhip.com) which one they suggest for printable PDF journals.
- After the first NC sale, open the receipt and check that NC sales tax shows. If it doesn't, tell Jarvis. Payhip would be the one to fix it, but it should be checked.
- Sell only through Payhip. Direct sales (a payment link outside Payhip, cash at a craft fair, an invoice to a counselor for a group license) are **not** covered. For those, NC would expect you to register (free, at ncdor.gov or sstregister.org) and collect tax yourself. Ask before making any sale outside Payhip.
- Keep your yearly Payhip sales report. Sales tax is separate from **income tax**: your profit is still income you report on your federal and NC returns (Schedule C).
- Free sampler downloads ($0) carry no sales tax.

## Why (the rules, with sources)

**Digital products are taxable in NC.** G.S. 105-164.3 defines "certain digital property" as specified digital products (digital audio works, digital audiovisual works, digital books) plus "additional digital goods": "A magazine, a newspaper, a newsletter, a report, or another publication," a photograph, and a greeting card, when transferred electronically. "Digital book" means "a work that is generally recognized in the ordinary and usual sense as a book that is transferred electronically." G.S. 105-164.4(a)(1)b applies the general rate to certain digital property sold at retail.
- G.S. 105-164.3 (definitions): https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-164.3.html
- G.S. 105-164.4 (tax imposed): https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-164.4.pdf
- NCDOR, Certain Digital Property: https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax/certain-digital-property
- NCDOR, Sales and Use Tax Bulletins (Section 23 covers digital property): https://www.ncdor.gov/sutb-2026pdf/open

Gray area: a printable journal is not named word for word. It is closest to a digital book or "another publication". A paper journal would be taxable too, so the safe reading is taxable. Payhip handles the collection either way, so this doesn't change what Tim does.

**Marketplace facilitators collect, not the seller.** NCDOR's Marketplace Facilitators and Marketplace Sellers FAQ (revised 4/30/2021):
- "Is a marketplace seller who only sells through a marketplace facilitator but has a physical presence in North Carolina required to register and file a return if the marketplace facilitator is required to collect and remit tax on all the marketplace seller's transactions? No. However, a marketplace seller who has a physical presence in North Carolina is required to register and remit use tax, as applicable."
- A marketplace seller with direct sales outside the marketplace must collect on those direct sales.
- Registration is free (online, Form NC-BR, or sstregister.org).
- FAQ PDF: https://www.ncdor.gov/faqsmf-msrevised04302021pdf/open
- FAQ page: https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax/marketplace-facilitators-and-marketplace-sellers/marketplace-facilitators-and-marketplace-sellers-frequently-asked-questions

"Use tax, as applicable" means tax on things a business buys for its own use without paying sales tax (for example, supplies bought from an out-of-state site that didn't charge tax). For a digital-only seller this is usually nothing. Keep it in mind if you buy business supplies tax-free.

- Creators set a product tax category under Advanced options and choose a tax display setting.

- US Sales Tax: https://help.payhip.com/article/392-us-sales-tax

**Thresholds (for reference).** NC's remote-seller threshold is more than $100,000 in NC sales in the current or prior year. The 200-transaction test was repealed effective July 1, 2024. Tim is an NC seller, so the threshold doesn't decide anything for him. What matters is that Payhip collects.
- NCDOR, Who Should Register: https://www.ncdor.gov/who-should-register-sales-and-use-tax
