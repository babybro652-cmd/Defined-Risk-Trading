# SBA 7(a) acquisition guide: apartment building vs laundromat (10/22/2026)

Prepared for Tim Williams by the Wealth agent. Education only, not financial, tax or legal advice. Nobody was contacted and nothing was applied for. A CPA and an attorney should review entity setup, taxes and any purchase contract before you sign.

## Bottom line

- **Apartment building (5+ units): SBA 7(a) is off the table.** The current SBA rulebook (SOP 50 10 8.1, in effect since October 1, 2026) says in plain words: "Apartment buildings and mobile home parks are not eligible." There is no workaround for a building you buy to rent to tenants.
- **Laundromat: SBA 7(a) fits.** Buying an existing laundromat is a normal "Initial Acquisition" under the SBA rules. Minimum down payment is 10% of total project cost, and half of that 10% can be a seller note on full standby.
- **Which path fits you now:** the laundromat, but only after two or three months of cleanup. With OneMain and Spotloan balances still being caught up, a lender will look hard at your credit and cash. Use the next 30 days to clean up credit, learn the business and build a deal file. For apartments, the realistic first step is a 2 to 4 unit owner-occupied house hack (FHA 3.5% down or Fannie Mae 5% down), not a 5+ unit building.

## 1. Apartment building (5+ units) with SBA 7(a)

### What the rule says

From SOP 50 10 8.1, Section A, Chapter 2, "Passive Businesses 13 CFR § 120.110(c)":

- "Businesses owned by developers and landlords that do not actively use or occupy the assets acquired or improved with the loan proceeds are not eligible, except Eligible Passive Companies under 13 CFR § 120.111."
- "Businesses that are primarily engaged in owning or purchasing real estate and leasing it for any purpose are not eligible."
- "Apartment buildings and mobile home parks are not eligible."
- "Residential facilities that are not licensed as nursing homes or assisted living facilities and do not provide healthcare and/or medical services are not eligible."

### The narrow exceptions (none fit a normal apartment building)

1. **Short-stay lodging.** Hotels, motels, RV parks, campgrounds and similar are eligible if more than 50% of last year's revenue came from guests staying 30 days or less. A 6-unit building rented on yearly leases does not qualify. Turning one into short-term rentals depends on Roxboro and Person County zoning, and it is a hotel business, not an apartment investment.
2. **Licensed care homes.** Nursing homes and assisted living with a state license and medical services are eligible.
3. **Owner-occupied commercial property.** An operating business can buy a building if it occupies at least 51% of an existing building and leases out up to 49%. Example: your laundromat buys a building and occupies the ground floor, with apartments upstairs, as long as the business occupies 51% of the rentable square footage.
4. **Eligible Passive Company (EPC).** A real estate LLC can borrow only if it leases 100% of the property to an operating company (your own business). Fantasy Homes LLC could own a laundromat's building and lease it to the laundromat LLC. It cannot own an apartment building under this rule.

**Conclusion:** the 7(a) route is closed for a 5+ unit apartment building. The SBA 504 program has the same passive-business rule.

### What lenders want from a new LLC

- **Personal guarantee** from you. A brand-new LLC has no credit, so the lender underwrites you.
- **Credit score.** Banks often want 680+; DSCR lenders 620 to 680+.
- **Reserves.** 3 to 12 months of principal, interest, taxes and insurance in the bank after closing.
- **Experience.** A property manager contract can substitute for some experience.
- **Entity status.** Fantasy Homes LLC's current NC status is not confirmed (the 2016 EIN notice reads "Fantasy Company LLC"). Check the NC Secretary of State site before using it, or form a new LLC. A CPA should sign off on the structure.

### Example: a 6-unit building in rural NC (assumptions, not a real listing)

Assumptions: price $480,000; rent $900 per unit; 7% vacancy; operating expenses 40% of collected rent; 25% down; 7.5% rate; 25-year amortization; $15,000 closing costs.

- Gross rent: 6 x $900 x 12 = $64,800
- Collected rent after 7% vacancy: $60,264
- Net operating income (NOI) after 40% expenses: $36,158
- Cap rate: $36,158 / $480,000 = **7.5%**
- Loan: $360,000. Annual debt service: **$31,924**
- DSCR: $36,158 / $31,924 = **1.13x** (below a bank's 1.20x to 1.25x)
- Cash flow after debt: $4,234 per year
- Cash-on-cash: $4,234 / ($120,000 + $15,000) = **3.1%**

Read: at 25% down this deal fails a bank's DSCR test and needs about $135,000 cash. It works only at a lower price, higher rents, or with seller financing. That is why a 5+ unit building is not the first move right now.

## 2. Laundromat with SBA 7(a)

All rules below are from SOP 50 10 8.1 (Appendix 15, "7(a) Changes of Ownership," and Section A) unless noted.

### Eligibility

- A laundromat is an operating business, so it is eligible. Buying an existing one is an **Initial Acquisition**: a new majority owner who was not previously an owner or employee.
- **New company as buyer is normal.** Form a new LLC (or use Fantasy Homes LLC if the CPA agrees and its status is current) to buy the assets. You guarantee the loan.
- **Startup laundromats are harder.** No history means the lender relies on projections and wants more experience and cash. Buy an existing store with 2 to 3 years of tax returns.
- **Ownership.** 100% of owners must be eligible persons (U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals living in the U.S.). Green card holders are now listed as ineligible owners under SOP 50 10 8.1.

### Equity injection (down payment)

- **Minimum 10% of total project cost** (price plus working capital, closing costs and other uses of proceeds). For an Initial Acquisition the SOP says it "cannot be reduced or eliminated."
- **Seller note can count for up to half** of the required 10%, only if it is on **full standby**: no principal or interest payments for the whole life of the SBA loan. It can accrue interest and be paid after the SBA loan is paid off.
- Other "limited" sources (standby debt from others, a minority investor under 20%) share that same 50% cap.
- **Unlimited sources:** your own cash that is not borrowed; or cash from a personal loan if you can show it will be repaid from something other than the business (your W-2 salary would count). Given your current debts, do not add a personal loan for this.
- **Partial seller financing not on standby** (seller paid monthly) is allowed as extra financing on top of your 10%, but the total debt cannot exceed the business valuation and must pass the DSCR test, and interest-only seller debt is underwritten on a 10-year amortization.
- **Seller earnouts are banned.** The seller can stay on only as a paid consultant for up to 24 months.

### Credit and underwriting

- **DSCR:** 1.25x for an Initial Acquisition, measured on last fiscal year or the 2-year average of historical EBITDA. Projections cannot be used to meet it.
- **Global cash flow** (you plus the business) must cover 1.0x. Your W-2 helps here.
- **Business valuation** by an accredited appraiser hired by the lender is required. If the price is above the valuation, you pay the gap in cash. A Quality of Earnings report is required only at $3M+.
- **Seller's numbers are checked against IRS tax transcripts**, and the lender does a site visit.
- **Credit score:** SBA retired the SBSS score requirement for small loans on March 1, 2026 and does not set a minimum consumer score for standard 7(a). Lenders use their own policies; plan on needing roughly 680+ and no recent late payments for an acquisition loan.
- **Personal guarantee:** required from anyone owning 20% or more.
- **Collateral:** business assets first. If the loan is not fully secured, the lender must take available equity in personal real estate of 20%+ owners, and life insurance for the collateral shortfall is required for a single-owner business. You rent, so this mostly means the business assets plus life insurance.

### Real estate included vs leased

- **Leased store:** for 7(a), the lease term including renewal options only you can exercise "should equal or exceed the term of the loan" (10 years), and must if the lender cannot get a lease assignment and landlord's waiver. Get a new or extended lease signed before closing.
- **Store building included:** the real estate can be on up to a 25-year term and the business part on 10 years, blended or split into two loans. The 10% equity applies to the real estate too. Fantasy Homes LLC could hold the building as an Eligible Passive Company leasing it to the laundromat LLC (CPA and attorney to sign off).

### Amounts, terms and fees

- **Max loan:** $5,000,000 for standard 7(a).
- **Terms:** 10 years for the business purchase, equipment and working capital; up to 25 years for real estate. No balloons.
- **Max rates (variable, prime-based):** prime + 6.5% for $50,000 or less, prime + 6.0% for $50,001 to $250,000, prime + 4.5% for $250,001 to $350,000, and prime + 3.0% over $350,000. With prime at 6.75% (WSJ prime unchanged since December 11, 2025, as of July 2026; check the current rate), the cap on a $300K loan is 11.25% and on a loan over $350K is 9.75%. Strong borrowers often price below the cap. Fixed-rate caps run 12% to 15% by size.
- **Upfront guaranty fee, FY2027** (loans approved 10/1/2026 to 9/30/2027, SBA Information Notice 5000-881797): 2% of the guaranteed part up to $150,000; 3% for $150,001 to $700,000; 3.5% to 3.75% above that. **0% for loans of $700,000 or less to businesses in SBA-designated rural areas** (SBA decides from the address in its E-Tran system). A Person County store could qualify; ask the lender to check the address.
- **Other costs:** business valuation, legal, environmental if real estate, and packaging fees.

### How long it takes

About 45 to 90 days from signed letter of intent to closing with a Preferred Lender. Reported average for Live Oak is about 45 days.

### Keeping your W-2 job

Yes, possible, but the lender must document "how the daily operations will be managed if the principals are not there on a daily basis." Have a written manager plan: an attendant or manager on payroll, cameras, card or app payment reporting, and your weekly hours (evenings and weekends). Lenders prefer buyers with some industry experience; a 24-month seller consulting agreement and an attendant who stays with the store help. A management company with sole control would make the business an ineligible passive business; you must keep control of the budget, bank accounts and employees.

### Due diligence must-dos

1. **Verify revenue with the water bill.** Laundromats run on cash. Get 24 to 36 months of water and sewer bills, convert gallons to turns per machine per day, and compare to reported revenue. Check gas and electric bills the same way.
2. **Tax returns vs claimed cash flow.** The lender checks returns against IRS transcripts. If the seller says "I take cash home," that income does not count.
3. **Equipment age and condition.** List every washer and dryer with brand, model, year and condition. Machines 18+ years old can mean a $50,000 to $300,000 replacement bill. Have a local distributor technician inspect.
4. **Lease.** At least 10 years remaining with your options, rent increases, who pays for roof, HVAC, water heater and sewer line, and the landlord's consent to assignment.
5. **Competition.** Map every laundromat within 3 to 5 miles; check for new stores planned.
6. **Coin vs card or app.** Card and app systems give clean sales records and support price increases, and they make lender verification easier. A coin-only store is cheaper and gives a value-add, budget $20,000 to $60,000 to convert (estimate; get distributor quotes).
7. **Extra income.** Wash-dry-fold, pickup and delivery, vending. These are where a part-time owner can grow revenue.
8. **Utilities and water heater.** Get the boiler or water heater age; it is a large repair item.
9. **Environmental.** If dry cleaning was ever on site, require an environmental review.

### Price multiples

- BizBuySell data (855 sold listings, 2021 to 2025): median **3.5x** seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), average 3.65x.
- Asking prices run higher (one October 2026 data set puts the median asking multiple near 4.7x).
- Newer equipment and a long lease push toward 4x to 5x; old equipment and a short lease push toward 2.5x to 3x.

### Sample deal: rural Person County laundromat (assumptions, not a real listing)

Assumptions: asset purchase, leased building with a 10-year lease plus options, SDE $90,000 (verified on tax returns and water bills), price $300,000 (3.3x SDE), SBA loan at 9.75% variable for 10 years, 75% SBA guaranty, 3% guaranty fee (0% if the address is SBA-rural).

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | $300,000 |
| Working capital | $15,000 |
| Closing costs (valuation, legal, packaging) | $10,000 |
| **Total project cost** | **$325,000** |
| Required equity (10%) | $32,500 |
| Of which: seller note on full standby (max half) | $16,250 |
| Of which: your cash | $16,250 |
| SBA loan before fee | $292,500 |
| Guaranty fee (3% of $219,375 guaranteed part), financed | $6,581 |
| **SBA loan** | **$299,081** |
| Monthly payment (9.75%, 120 months) | **$3,911** |
| Annual debt service | $46,933 |

Cash flow:

| | Absentee (manager paid $25,000) | You work weekends (help paid $12,000) |
|---|---|---|
| SDE | $90,000 | $90,000 |
| Manager or attendant pay | -$25,000 | -$12,000 |
| Equipment reserve | -$6,000 | -$6,000 |
| Cash flow before debt | $59,000 | $72,000 |
| Debt service | -$46,933 | -$46,933 |
| **Cash flow to you, before tax** | **$12,067 per year (about $1,006 per month)** | **$25,067 per year (about $2,089 per month)** |
| DSCR on this cash flow | 1.26x | 1.53x |

Cash you need at closing: about $16,250, plus 3 to 6 months of payments in personal reserves ($12,000 to $24,000) that lenders like to see. Cash-on-cash in the absentee case: $12,067 / $16,250 = 74%; this is high because the seller standby note and SBA loan carry most of the price. A $20,000 equipment repair in year one would wipe out the absentee case's year of cash flow, which is why the equipment inspection matters.

## 3. Best places to shop

### Laundromats

1. **BizBuySell** (bizbuysell.com, category "Laundromats and Coin Laundries," filter North Carolina) and **BizQuest** (bizquest.com, coin laundry listings by NC county, including Raleigh-Durham and Wake County).
2. **Local equipment distributors** (Speed Queen, Electrolux, Dexter, Continental dealers serving the Triangle). They service the stores and know which owners want out, often before a listing.
3. **Raleigh-Durham business brokers** shown on BizQuest laundry listings: First Choice Business Brokers (Raleigh-Durham), Murphy Business Sales (Raleigh), Transworld Business Advisors of Central NC, Sunbelt Business Brokers (Raleigh), ENLIGN Business Brokers. Confirm each one's laundromat track record first.
4. **Laundromat-specific sources:** Coin Laundry Association (coinlaundry.org) for training, industry data and member listings; Laundromat Resource (podcast, courses and buyer community); LoopNet for store-plus-building listings.
5. **Direct outreach:** visit stores in Roxboro, Oxford, Yanceyville, Durham and South Boston VA; owners with old machines and handwritten signs are often ready to sell.

### 5+ unit buildings

1. **LoopNet** and **Crexi** (filter multifamily, 5+ units, Person, Granville, Durham, Vance, Caswell counties).
2. **Local commercial brokers** in Durham and Roxboro who handle small multifamily; ask to be on their pre-market list.
3. **Zillow and Realtor.com** multi-family filters (5+ unit buildings sometimes show up on the MLS).
4. **Off-market:** driving for dollars; Person County GIS and Register of Deeds to find owners of 5+ unit parcels (look for long ownership, out-of-state mailing address, no mortgage); mail a short letter.
5. **Tax lien and foreclosure lists** from the county tax office (ties to your tax lien plan).

## 5. Next 30 days

1. **Week 1: credit and cash.** Pull all three credit reports (annualcreditreport.com). Keep OneMain and Spotloan current; no new debt. Set a target: $20,000 to $35,000 saved for equity plus reserves.
2. **Week 1: entity check.** Look up Fantasy Homes LLC on the NC Secretary of State site; note status and annual report dues. Ask a CPA whether to use it or form a new laundromat LLC.
3. **Week 2: learn the business.** Visit 5 laundromats within 30 minutes (count machines, prices, payment system, condition, hours). Join or browse Coin Laundry Association material.
4. **Week 2: set listing alerts.** BizBuySell and BizQuest (NC laundromats, under $600K); LoopNet and Crexi (5 to 20 units, your counties).
5. **Week 3: talk to a distributor.** Ask which stores are for sale or aging, and what equipment and card conversions cost.
6. **Week 3: lender groundwork.** Run SBA Lender Match (it is a form, not an application); read Live Oak, Huntington and Newtek laundromat pages. Do not apply yet.
7. **Week 4: build a deal file.** Personal financial statement, 3 years of tax returns, resume showing management experience, a one-page manager plan for running the store while working 6:00 AM to 4:30 PM.
8. **Week 4: screen one real listing** with this guide's math: price / SDE, DSCR at 1.25x, lease length, equipment age, water bills.
9. **House hack track:** get a pre-qualification estimate for an FHA or Fannie 5% duplex to triplex in Roxboro once credit is cleaned up. This is the apartment path.
