## 4.3: Fixed Range Volume Profile Basics

**TELL:** "This tool answers a different question than price alone. Not 'where did price go' but 'where did the most trading actually happen.' POC, the Point of Control, is the single price with the most volume. The value area is the zone holding about 70% of the volume around it. That's TradingView's default setting."

## 4.4: Using Volume to Confirm Zones

**TELL:** "High-volume areas mean price 'agrees' it belongs there. It might pause or reverse, and it's harder for a sweep to punch through cleanly. Low-volume gaps mean price moved through fast, without much disagreement. That's where I often see manipulation sweeps happen, because there's less resistance."

## 4.5: Using the POC as an Entry or Exit **[v3: new lesson]**

**TELL:** "In 4.4 we used volume to judge the sweep. Now I'm going to show you how I use one line off that profile, the POC, as my entry or my exit. This is how I trade it. It's not a law the market has to follow.

First, the POC as my exit. I draw the Fixed Range Volume Profile on the 30-minute chart, over the range I'm trading. After the sweep and my CRT entry, I manage the trade at the POC of that range, or a few ticks before it. Sometimes I take the full exit there. Sometimes I take a partial and let the rest run. Which one depends on how the candles are moving into the POC. That's a read, not a formula: watch how price travels into that level, and you'll build the feel for it with screen time. The POC is where the most trading happened in that range, so that's where I expect price to stall. Session liquidity tells me where price is headed. The POC tells me where it's likely to slow down on the way.

Second, the POC as my entry. When price leaves the range and comes back to the POC, I watch how it reacts there. I don't enter on the touch. I wait for a reaction candle at the POC, and if it lines up with my CRT direction, I enter on that candle's close. Now your stop. I manage my own trades by hand, but that's me, not you. You use a hard stop of 3.5 to 4 points on ES. On a POC entry, that's 3.5 to 4 points from your entry, the close of the reaction candle. If you're a beginner, you don't take a trade without a stop. No exceptions. We cover position size and risk in detail in Module 5. My target is the session high or the session low, whichever is the untaken liquidity in my trade direction. I exit at that line or within a point of it.

Same as everything in this module: one line doesn't make the trade. The POC has to fit the setup."

**SHOW:** Start on the example slide (synthetic candles on the 30-minute chart, labeled Example, not a real trade). Left: sweep under the range low, CRT entry, target at the POC. Right: price leaves the range, pulls back to the POC, prints a reaction candle, entry on that candle's close, hard stop 3.5 to 4 points below the entry, target at the untaken session high. Then switch to the live chart, draw Fixed Range Volume Profile on the 30-minute chart over the range you trade, and point to the POC. Show one setup where the POC was the target and one where it was the entry.

**DO:** "On the 30-minute chart, draw Volume Profile on the range you're trading and mark the POC. Then pull up your Module 3 CRT screenshot and mark the POC on it: was it your entry or your target? Post it with your entry, stop and target marked."

# MODULE 5: Full Workflow & Risk Management

## 5.1: Pre-Trade Checklist

**TELL:** "This is the one thing I want glued to your monitor. Seven steps before any entry:
1. Confirm the AMD phase.
2. Check the higher-timeframe daily bias.
3. Confirm you're in your window. While you're learning, that's 10 AM to 12 PM Eastern.
4. Identify the session liquidity targets.
5. Check that volume profile agrees on the 30-minute chart. Mark the POC: is it your entry or your target? **[v3]**
6. Confirm CRT confluence.
7. Define your risk before entry.

Every single time. No skipping steps because you're excited."

**SHOW:** Switch to the live chart. Run the 7-step checklist out loud on a recent setup and check off each item as you go.

**DO:** "Print or save this checklist somewhere you'll see it before every trade. Post a photo of wherever you put it: screen, wall, notebook, doesn't matter."

## 5.2: Position Sizing & Daily Risk Rules

**TELL (the formula):** "Here's the math in plain terms. Decide how many dollars you're willing to risk on this one trade. Divide that by how many points your stop is away from entry. Divide by $50, the ES point value. That's the formula professional position sizing is built on.

**TELL (your actual size):** But that's not what you'll use day to day while you're in this program. I'm giving you two options instead. Pick one and keep it fixed, every trade. Option A: 1 ES contract. With the 3.5 to 4 point hard stop, one stopped trade is $175 to $200, inside the $200 early-session limit. Option B: 2 ES contracts, every point is $100, and your daily limit goes up to $400. One stopped trade is $350 to $400, so that one loss ends your session. Here's the trade-off: A gives you more attempts. B makes bigger moves, but only one loss is allowed. It's your choice. Stay with it until your account has grown 1.5x to 2x your starting capital. Once you hit that milestone, you can size up, to a max of 4.

**TELL (your daily ceiling):** Know your number before you trade. On Option A, your hard stop risks $175 to $200 per trade. On Option B, it's $350 to $400, and one loss ends your session. Do the quick math for your option: how many losing trades would it take to hit your daily limit? If you don't know that number before you start trading, you're flying blind. Once you can answer it without doing math in the moment, you're ready to size up, to a max of 4 contracts.

**TELL (my numbers):** Here are my numbers. I never risk more than $1,000 total in a single day, and no more than $200 of that during the manipulation session, 3:00 to 9:29 AM, which I don't want you trading yet anyway. And my daily goal is 1 or 2 good trades, then I'm done. These are my numbers, not yours. Set your own daily cap before you trade, and in an eval, it's the firm's daily loss limit. More trades isn't better. It usually means you stopped waiting for the real setup.

**TELL (your profit goal):** For you, while you're learning, set a daily profit goal. Two ways: a flat $400 a day, or personalize it. Take your hourly rate, multiply by 8, then multiply that by 2. So at $20 an hour: $20 × 8 = $160, then × 2 = $320. It's a reference number, not a quota. You still stop after 1 or 2 good trades."

**SHOW:** Switch to the live chart. "3.5 to 4 point hard stop. Option A, 1 ES contract: that's $175 to $200 of risk on this trade. Option B, 2 ES contracts: $350 to $400, and one loss ends the session." Then show the profit goal next to it.

**DO:** "Three numbers, posted together. One: pick Option A (1 ES contract) or Option B (2 ES contracts), then, with the 3.5 to 4 point hard stop on a real setup from your chart, your dollar risk on that trade. Two: write down your daily limit, then how many losers like that one it allows. Three: your daily profit goal, either $400 flat or your hourly rate × 8 × 2."
