## Why Lucid over Apex for Tim

1. **Cash.** $80.50 one-time and no activation fee. Apex EOD at a good promo is still about $176-250 once activation is added, and the eval dies after 30 days, so a slow month means paying again.
2. **No 30-day clock.** A deadline pushes him to force trades, which is the London-account pattern. Lucid lets him sit out bad days.
3. **End-of-day drawdown.** His trades run, pull back, then go (10/29: ran, came back near entry, then +28 pts). An intraday trail would raise the floor on the open profit and stop him out of the good trades.
4. **The DLL is a real seatbelt.** $1,200 is a soft lock, not a failed account, and it sits just above his own $1,000 max.
5. **Faster, simpler first payout.** No buffer, no consistency rule once funded, and $500 out after about $1,000 profit. Apex needs $2,600 profit before the first $500.
6. **He knows the platform.**

Downsides: Lucid's **40% eval consistency** means a big day (like 11/9's +$2,700 on 2 ES) doesn't fail him, but it makes him keep trading until the best day is 40% or less of total profit. That's where overtrading starts. On 1 ES, a single day rarely passes $1,200, so the rule mostly stays out of the way. Also, Lucid is newer (founded 2025), and its 5-payout path to live is a judgment call by Lucid, not guaranteed.

## The one rule most likely to fail him

**The $2,000 end-of-day trailing max loss (the old $48K floor).**
- 11/9 lost $1,238 in one session: 62% of the whole drawdown in a day.
- The floor trails up after every green close until $52,100. A +$1,000 day moves the floor to $49,000, and a $1,238 day after it leaves about $1,760 of room on the next day.
- Fixes that are already his rules: 1 ES until the floor locks, 3.5-4 pt hard stop on the first entry, 1-2 trades, done at -$400 on 1 ES (not -$1,000), NY session only, nothing before 10 AM ET.
