Before the New York open, I draw 2 lines on the ES chart: the overnight high and the overnight low.

ES trades nearly 23 hours a day. When the 9:30 AM ET bell rings, the overnight hours have left a range behind. I read the top and bottom of that range as spots where stops pile up, and price tends to go looking for them.

That changes how I trade near them. When price gets close to one, I slow down. I don't chase a move into the line. I wait to see if my setup forms there, with a 3.5 to 4 point stop set before I click.

2 lines, drawn before the open. They keep me from guessing in the middle of nowhere.

## Facebook 5:00 PM (50185, page 993318963868124)

```
The trade right after a stop-out is the most dangerous one I can take.

The loss itself is fine. It was planned. I knew the stop before I entered, and the market took it. That's the cost of running this business.

The problem is the urge that shows up a few seconds later: get it back, right now. That click has no setup behind it. It's anger with a buy button.

So my rule is simple to say and hard to follow. Stop hit, I stand up and walk away from the chart. When I sit back down, I ask 1 question: is my setup still there? If it isn't, I'm done.

Send this to the trader in your life who clicks again too fast.
```
