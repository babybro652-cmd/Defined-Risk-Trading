## 1. The monetization thresholds (and a deadline that changes the math)

### Today (through Jan 31, 2027)
| Tier | Subs | Plus one of | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan funding / Shopping (lower tier) | 500 | 3,000 long-form watch hours in 12 mo, or 3M Shorts views in 90 days (plus 3 public uploads in 90 days) | Memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, Shopping. No ad revenue. |
| Full YPP (ad revenue) | 1,000 | 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 mo, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days | Watch-page ads + Shorts Feed ad share + Premium |

### From Feb 1, 2027 (announced Aug 10, 2026)
- New applicants to the ad tier: 1,000 subs plus **8,000 watch hours** in 365 days or **20M Shorts views** in 90 days.
- Lower tier (500 subs + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views) stays the same.
- Existing partners keep status but must accept new terms by Jan 31, 2027 and stay active.
- YouTube's own help page confirms the Feb 1, 2027 update date; the doubled numbers come from
  trade press quoting the Team YouTube post. Confirm in YouTube Studio > Earn before planning on them.

### Counting rules that matter
- Only **public** Shorts in the Shorts Feed count. Private, unlisted, deleted Shorts and ad-driven views don't.
- **Shorts Feed watch time does not count toward the long-form hours.** Hours come from long-form only.

### Realistic timeline for DRT (1 Short a day)
Baseline: DRT TikTok gets 180-450 views a post, FB reels about 200, one FB outlier hit 3,406.
A new YouTube channel posting 1 Short a day will likely sit in the hundreds of views per Short for
the first 1-2 months, with an occasional spike.

| Path | What it takes | Realistic for DRT |
|---|---|---|
| 3M Shorts views / 90 days (lower tier) | ~33,000 Shorts views a day, every day | Not from 1 Short/day at current reach. Needs repeated viral hits. Don't plan on it. |
| 10M or 20M Shorts views / 90 days | 111k-222k views a day | Off the table for 2027. |
| 500 subs + 3,000 long-form hours (lower tier) | e.g. 1 long-form a week, ~12 min, ~4 min average watched = 15 views per hour, so ~45,000 long-form views in 12 months (~870 per video at 52 videos) | **6-12 months** if Shorts feed the long-form and the long-form keeps people watching. This is the first real target. |
| 1,000 subs + 8,000 hours (ad tier after Feb 1, 2027) | ~120,000 long-form views a year at the same retention | **12-24 months.** The 4,000-hour bar ends before DRT can reach it. |

Bottom line: Shorts are the discovery engine. Long-form builds the hours and the trust that sells
Skool, and the money comes from that, not from Shorts ads.

### What Shorts and long-form pay (planning ranges)
- Finance Shorts RPM: about **$0.05-$0.35 per 1,000 views** (US-heavy audience at the top). 1M Shorts views = roughly $50-$350.
- Finance long-form RPM: about **$4-$12 conservative** (vidIQ), some sources quote $12-$40.
- Same 100k views: Shorts ~ $5-$35, long-form ~ $400-$1,200. Long-form is where AdSense lives.

### How Shorts feed long-form
- Each Short gets one "related video" link to a long-form video on the channel. Set it in
  YouTube Studio (Details > Related video) or in the mobile app at upload. **Blotato can't set it**
  (YouTube's API doesn't allow third-party tools to), so Tim adds it once long-form exists.
- Cut 3-5 Shorts from every long-form and link each back to it.
- Shorts viewers who subscribe get shown the long-form in their feed; the long-form is what
  converts into Skool members and affiliate clicks.

## 2. What works on trading Shorts and TikTok right now

There's no reliable public study of futures-trading formats. These picks combine general Shorts
data (hook in the first 1-2 seconds, readable on mute, completion rate) with DRT's own numbers.
DRT's breakout (3,406 FB views) was an education hook: "The market's first move of the day is
designed to trap retail traders." Lead with that style.

### Top formats for DRT (ranked)
1. **"Why I didn't take this trade" (no-trade breakdowns).** Tim's chart, the setup that almost
   fired, the rule that said no. Fits defined risk, needs no P&L, so it's autopilot-safe.
   Today's paper trading reel is this format.
2. **30-second chart breakdowns.** One concept on a real ES chart: the opening trap, the overnight
   range, a sweep and reclaim, where the stop goes. Circle it, label it, done.
3. **Myth busts.** "More screen time = more money" (no), "you need a 20-point stop on ES" (no),
   "paper trading is useless" (no). Contrarian line on screen in second 1.
4. **Prop firm eval series (numbered).** "Eval attempt, day 3: rules I trade by." Rules, the
   daily loss limit, what the trailing drawdown does, how Tim lost 2 accounts trading London and
   what changed. Series titles pull binge viewing. **Any real account numbers or P&L need Tim's go.**
5. **P&L-honest losses (with Tim's go).** A real stop-out at 3.5-4 points shown calmly: "this is
   what a defined-risk loss looks like." Loss content builds trust in a niche full of fake wins.
   Trade content, so held for Tim's approval every time.

Also good: live trade replays with the risk box drawn before entry (Tim's go), "my 3 rules before
the open" checklists, "trader dad" lifestyle (new dad only; **no W-2, 9-5, work hours or lunch
breaks**, per the 10/22 rule, so the "day in the life of a trader dad with a W-2" idea is out).

### Hook patterns that fit
- Contrarian: "Stop trading London." / "Your stop is too wide."
- Payoff first: "This is the only ES setup I take."
- Question the video answers: "Why did I sit out the whole day?"
- Show the chart in frame 1, text on screen in the top third, readable with sound off.
- No "hey guys", no intro, no logo sting.

### Retention, length, captions
- **Length:** 20-40 seconds for most Shorts. Under 60 s keeps them safely in the Shorts shelf
  (YouTube now allows Shorts up to 3 minutes, but shorter Shorts finish more often).
- **Loop:** end on a line that lands back on the hook so replays count.
- **Burned-in captions** on every Short (most people watch muted).
- One idea per Short. Cut every pause.
- **Title** under 60 characters with the keyword (ES, futures, prop firm, paper trading, stop) up front.
- **Description:** 1-2 lines + disclaimer + 2-3 hashtags incl. #shorts.

### Series and playlists
- Playlists: "Why I Didn't Take It", "ES in 30 Seconds", "Prop Firm Rules", "Trading Myths",
  "Weekly ES Breakdown" (long-form), "Defined Risk Basics" (long-form).
- Number series episodes in the title ("Ep. 4") so viewers go looking for the rest.

### Cadence
- 1 Short a day (automated, 6:12 PM ET).
- 1 long-form a week from Tim's existing market breakdown / trade recordings (he already records
  these for Skool).
- Optional: 1 live stream a month (NY open walkthrough, replay only, no signals) once the channel has 100+ subs.

### Posting time
2026 guides disagree on exact days; most put weekday Shorts reach in the 6-10 PM window, and all
of them say your own Studio data beats generic advice. We picked **6:12 PM ET**: after the 4 PM
cash close when US traders are off the chart, inside the evening window, off the round hour. After
30 days, check Studio > Analytics > Audience > "When your viewers are on YouTube" and move it.

## 3. Compliance (read before any post that mentions money, a prop firm or a link)

Not legal advice. If DRT starts selling signals, managing money or taking prop firm affiliate
money at scale, talk to a lawyer who knows CFTC/NFA rules.

**YouTube policy**
- Spam, deceptive practices and scams policy bans "get rich quick" content and misleading titles,
  thumbnails or descriptions. Three strikes in 90 days ends a channel.
- Content that breaks Community Guidelines or advertiser-friendly guidelines can be demonetized
  or block YPP approval. Keep thumbnails and titles honest.
- Any sponsorship or paid placement: tick "includes paid promotion" in YouTube Studio.

**FTC (affiliate links and sponsors)**
- Disclosure must be clear and conspicuous: hard to miss and easy to understand.
- Put it in the first lines of the description, above the links, and say it out loud in the
  video ("I earn a commission if you use my link"). A line at the end of a long description fails.
- A commission is a material connection even if Tim bought the product himself.

**Futures-specific**
- CFTC Rule 4.41 requires a set disclaimer next to any hypothetical or simulated results for
  CTAs and CPOs, and the CFTC has charged people who marketed on YouTube without it. Tim isn't a
  registered CTA, but if a video shows backtest, sim or indicator "results", put the hypothetical
  performance disclaimer on screen and in the description anyway.
- A vendor blog reports NFA Notice I-26-12 (Aug 30, 2026) on prop firm affiliate marketing,
  banning lines like "guaranteed funded account" or "risk-free challenge". Not confirmed on
  nfa.futures.org yet; follow it anyway.

**DRT rules for every video**
- Description line on every upload: "Education only. Not financial advice. Futures trading
  involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for every investor."
- Never: guaranteed returns, "easy money", income claims, "pass your eval in 2 days", signals
  ("buy here now"), invented stats, or personalized advice in comments.
- Real P&L, funded numbers, payouts: Tim's explicit go every time, and show losses as honestly as wins.
- No "AMD" or "CRT" anywhere, including the indicator's public name.
