# DRT LIVE: 30-minute evening show

2-3 nights a week (pick nights after his son's bedtime, Sun-Thu work best since they set up
the next trading day). This is a review show: no live trading on stream. The overnight ES
session (Asia, London) runs while it airs, and Tim doesn't trade those sessions.

Overlays: banner + disclaimer bar on the whole time, tagline strip on the whole time,
bias card in segment 2, plan card in segment 4. Setup: `drt/live/setup-guide.md`.

## Safe-talk rules (read before every show)

1. **No signals.** Never "buy here", "short at X", "take this trade tomorrow". Say "this is
   the level I'm watching and why" and "this is what I'd need to see first".
2. **No P&L promises or income claims.** No "you can make $X a day", no account sizes or
   payout totals to hype anyone. If someone asks how much he makes: "I track R, not dollars,
   and results vary. Most people lose money trading."
3. **Paper vs live honesty.** Say which it was every time: "That was paper" or "That was a
   live account." Never let a paper result sound like real money.
4. **Disclaimer out loud** three times: at the start, around minute 15, and at the sign-off.
   "This is education only, not financial advice. Futures carry real risk."
5. **No acronyms for the model.** Say "the 3-candle model", "the sweep", "session
   liquidity". Indicator names stay hidden on the chart.
6. **Day job facts only.** "I work a full-time job and trade the New York session around
   it." He can mention trading on his lunch break sometimes and that phones aren't allowed
   on the floor after. Never name the employer or site, never say he quit, never invent
   schedule details. Nothing that sounds like trading on company time.
7. **Own the losses.** Show red days the same way as green days. A rule break gets named as
   a rule break.
8. **Q&A boundaries.** No advice on someone's account, broker, prop firm payout or position.
   Redirect: "I can't tell you what to do with your money. Here's how I'd think about the
   risk."
9. **Skool mention once,** calm, at the end. No pressure, no prices, no countdowns.

## Run of show

### 0:00-2:00 Intro and hook (2 min)
- Open on one line about today's market, not "hey guys": "ES swept yesterday's low at the
  open and reversed. Here's what that sweep told me."
- Who he is in one sentence: "I'm Tim, I trade ES futures in the New York session around a
  full-time job, and every night I break down the day and plan the next one."
- Disclaimer out loud #1.
- What's coming: recap, my trade or why I sat out, tomorrow's plan, your questions.

### 2:00-10:00 Today's market recap with the day's levels (8 min)
Bias card on (`TODAY'S BIAS: ____`). Walk the chart left to right, slow.
- Higher timeframe bias coming into the day and whether it held.
- The levels, drawn before the show:
  - Prior day high / low
  - Overnight (Asia and London) high / low
  - New York open price and the first 15-30 minute range
  - Volume profile point of control / value area edges, if used that day
- What liquidity got taken (which high or low swept), and when, in ET.
- The 3-candle setup if one formed: where it would be valid, where it failed.
- One sentence lesson to close the segment.

### 10:00-15:00 His trade, or why he sat out (5 min)
- If he traded: entry, stop, target, result in points and R. Say paper or live. Was the
  stop within the 3.5-4 point rule? Was it at or after 10 AM ET? Did he wait for the label?
- If he sat out: what he was waiting for and why it never showed. Sitting out is a result.
- Rules check out loud: NY session only, no trades before 10 AM ET, wait for the label,
  3.5-4 pt hard stop, 1-2 good trades then done, max $1,000 risk in a day.
- Disclaimer out loud #2 at the end of this segment (about minute 15).

### 15:00-23:00 Tomorrow's plan and bias prep (8 min)
Swap to the `TOMORROW'S PLAN` card.
- Bias for tomorrow and what would flip it.
- Levels to watch at the New York open (mark them live on the chart).
- Scheduled news that could move ES (check the economic calendar before the show; name
  the event and time ET, no predictions).
- "If / then" scenarios, never orders: "If price sweeps the overnight low and the 3-candle
  model forms after 10, that's my kind of setup. If it runs straight through, I sit out."
- Close with the plan in one line on screen.

### 23:00-28:00 Q&A (5 min)
- Pick questions about process, risk, levels, routine, paper trading, prop firm prep.
- Skip or redirect: "what should I buy", account-specific questions, "how much do you make".
- If chat is quiet, answer a pre-picked question: "How do you size a stop?", "Why no trades
  before 10?", "How long did you paper trade?"

### 28:00-30:00 Skool CTA and sign-off (2 min)
- One calm line: "The full process, the paper trading track and the trade reviews live in
  the Skool community. Link's in my bio."
- Disclaimer out loud #3.
- Sign-off: **"Define your risk, wait for your setup."**

## Prep (15 minutes before)
- Draw today's levels and tomorrow's levels on the chart.
- Pick the bias card.
- Write the one-line hook and the one-line lesson.
- Know the answer to: did I trade, paper or live, result in R, rules kept?
