## Public-copy rule (read first)
**Never name "AMD" or "CRT" in any public post, caption, or video script.** They're Tim's proprietary method names and stay inside the paid course. Describe the idea instead: "the 3-candle entry", "the sweep before the real move", "session liquidity", "killzones".

## Business
Defined Risk Trading is an ES futures trading education and mentorship program run by Tim Williams on Skool. It teaches a structured, repeatable process: a session-based market-cycle framework, a 3-candle entry model, killzones, higher-timeframe daily bias, session liquidity, and volume profile confluence. It's delivered as a curriculum (Modules 1-5, a required Paper Trading track, and Prop Firm Readiness) with live trade review sessions, not just pre-recorded videos.

## Customer
Retail futures traders serious enough to invest in structured mentorship rather than consume free content. They've been trading undisciplined or unprofitably, and they want a defined-risk framework, real accountability, and a live cohort/community to work through it with, not another strategy PDF they'll skim once. Many work a 9-5 and trade around it.

## Strong Opinion / Wedge
Most trading "gurus" skip risk management entirely. They sell entries and strategies but never teach position sizing or daily loss limits, so students blow up anyway even when the setup was right. Defined Risk Trading requires 20 paper trades over a minimum of 2 weeks, every one following the full pre-trade checklist with risk defined before entry, before touching real money.

Second wedge: most traders don't have a strategy problem, they have a discipline problem. They have a workable system and override it.

## Voice

## Universal Voice Rules (apply to every post)
- Contractions always ("don't" not "do not")
- Active voice, short sentences
- Address the reader as "you"
- Numbers as digits ("3 tips" not "three tips")
- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or "..."
- One concrete idea per post, not three
- Specific details over generic statements
- Every platform gets its own caption. Never reuse the same wording across platforms.
- No filler words: actually, really, just, simply, truly, genuinely
- Lead with the result or the concept, not a confession. 9-5 proof posts and education posts perform best
