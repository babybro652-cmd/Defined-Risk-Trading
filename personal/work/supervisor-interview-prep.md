## 1. What an inventory supervisor owns (know this cold)
- **Inventory accuracy:** what the system says matches what's on the shelf. Usually measured as location accuracy and unit accuracy (many DCs target 98% to 99.5%+).
- **Cycle counts:** daily counts of a slice of locations instead of shutting down for one big count. Often ABC: A items (fast movers, high value) counted most often.
- **Variance research:** when a count is off, find WHY before adjusting. Mispick, mis-putaway, receiving error, damage not recorded, theft, unit-of-measure mix-up (each vs case).
- **Adjustments:** approving and documenting them. Big-dollar adjustments get a second count and a manager sign-off.
- **Shrink and damages:** tracking losses, damage process, returns to vendor.
- **Physical inventory / audits:** planning the annual or quarterly count, count teams, recounts, cutoff (stopping receiving and shipping so numbers freeze).
- **Replenishment and location control:** pick faces stay full, no "ghost" inventory, empty locations really empty.
- **The team:** inventory control clerks or counters, their training, rate, and accuracy.
- **Know your WMS by name** ([system name]) and the reports you'd use: count variance, adjustment history, short picks, empty-location audit.

"I've been here [X years] in [department]. I train associates on the order picker, and I've been sent to

- Most inventory errors show up at the pick face. You've seen them firsthand: [examples: short picks, wrong item in slot, overflow not in the system].
- You train pickers, so you can train counters and fix the habits that cause variances (scanning the location, not grabbing from the next slot, reporting damage).
- Training at other sites shows you can set up a process and get people to follow it.

## 4. Your 5 stories (STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result)
Keep each under 2 minutes. End with a number if you have one.

1. **Found and fixed an inventory problem.** A short pick or wrong-slot issue you caught, how you traced it, what you did. Result: [fixed location, stopped repeat errors].
2. **Training someone who struggled.** What you changed in how you taught them. Result: [hit rate or accuracy in X days].
3. **Sister DC trip.** Why they sent you, what was broken, what you set up. Result: [their numbers after].
4. **Attention to detail / doing it right under pressure.** Volume spike or short staffed, and you didn't cut corners. Result.
5. **Conflict or correcting a coworker.** Someone skipping a step (not scanning, unsafe). How you handled it. Result.

## 5. Questions they'll likely ask (with your answer)
- **A cycle count is off by 40 units. What do you do?** Recount first. Check recent transactions on that SKU and location: receipts, putaways, picks, adjustments. Check nearby locations and overflow for the product. Check unit of measure. Find the cause, then adjust with notes, and fix the cause (retrain, relabel, fix the slot).
- **How do you improve inventory accuracy?** Cycle count A items more often, root cause every variance, track the top error causes weekly, retrain on the top one, audit locations (empty and pick faces), hold people accountable for scanning.
- **What causes inventory variances?** Mispicks, mis-putaways, receiving miscounts, unrecorded damage, unit-of-measure errors, theft, system timing (a count during an open transaction).
- **How would you plan a physical inventory?** Set cutoff, clean and label locations ahead, pre-count hard areas, two-person count teams, blind counts (counters don't see the system number), recount anything over the threshold, reconcile before reopening.
- **A counter keeps making errors. What do you do?** Find out why (training, rushing, equipment). Retrain, pair with a strong counter, audit their counts, set a goal and date, document, follow policy if it doesn't improve.
- **How will you supervise people who are your friends now?** Same rules for everyone. Tell them straight on day one that the job changed. Fair and consistent, not friendly-first.
- **Which numbers would you track?** Location accuracy, unit accuracy, adjustment dollars, shrink, count completion vs schedule, variance by cause, short picks.
- **Tell me about a mistake you made and what you learned.**
- **Where do you see yourself in 3 to 5 years?**

## 6. Questions to ask them
**Top 3 (if you only get a few minutes)**
1. "What's our inventory accuracy now, and where do you want it in 6 months?" Shows you think in numbers.
2. "What's the biggest cause of our variances right now?" Shows you the real problem, and you can answer it with your picker experience.
3. "What would make someone a success in this role in the first 90 days?" Tells you exactly what they'll grade you on.

**Extras if there's time**
- How big is the inventory team, and which shifts does it cover?
- How are cycle counts scheduled now? Do we count fast movers more often?
- Who approves adjustments, and what's the dollar limit before a manager signs off?
- What training do new supervisors get?
- What did the last person in this role do well, and where did they struggle?

**Always close with:** "Is there anything about my background that makes you hesitate? I'd like the chance to answer it." It gets their doubts out while you're still in the room.

**Hold for the offer (or HR):** pay, schedule, time off.

## 7. Night before / day of
- Know your numbers: your rate, accuracy, how many people you've trained, which sites you traveled to.
- If you can, pull up the WMS reports you'd use so you can name them.
- Dress a step above floor clothes unless told otherwise. Arrive 10 minutes early.
- Bring a notepad with your 5 stories as bullets and your 3 questions.
- Slow down when you answer. Pausing before an answer is fine.
- Same day: a 2-sentence thank-you to the interviewer with one specific thing from the talk.

## 8. Don'ts
- Don't trash a current supervisor or coworker.
- Don't bring up side businesses or trading.
- Don't say "I'll just adjust it to match." Always: recount, research, then adjust.
- Don't guess numbers. "I don't have the exact number, but it was around X" is fine.
