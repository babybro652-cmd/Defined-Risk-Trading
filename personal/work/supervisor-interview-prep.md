# Supervisor Interview Prep (started 11/5/2026)

Role: supervisor at Tim's distribution center (internal promotion).
Known facts: full-time 6 AM to 4:30 PM, trains order picker skills, has traveled to sister DCs to train teams.
Fill in the [brackets] with your real numbers and names. Never guess a number in the room.

## 1. Your pitch (30 seconds, "Tell me about yourself")
"I've been here [X years] in [department]. Besides my own work, I train new associates on the order picker,
and I've been sent to [sister DC names] to train their teams. The part I like most is getting someone
from nervous on day one to running on their own, safely and on rate. I'm going for supervisor because
I already do part of the job. I want to own the whole shift's results: safety, rate, and people."

## 2. Your 5 stories (STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result)
Have one ready for each. Keep each under 2 minutes. End with a number if you have one.

1. **Training someone who struggled.** A trainee who was slow or unsafe on the picker. What you changed in how you taught them. Result: [hit rate in X days / passed certification].
2. **Sister DC trip.** Why they sent you, what was broken there, what you set up. Result: [their rate, errors or injuries after].
3. **Safety call.** A time you stopped unsafe work or corrected someone, even when it slowed things down. Result: nobody hurt, and how the person took it.
4. **Conflict.** Two coworkers, or you and a coworker, didn't get along. How you handled it without going to a manager first. Result.
5. **Pressure day.** Short staffed, truck volume spike, system down. What you did to get the work out. Result: [trucks shipped on time, overtime avoided].

## 3. Questions they'll likely ask
- Why do you want to be a supervisor?
- How will you handle supervising people who are your friends now? (Have this one ready. Answer: same rules for everyone, you'll tell them straight on day one that the job changes, and you'll be fair, not friendly-first.)
- A good worker keeps missing rate. What do you do? (Find out why first: training, equipment, personal issue. Retrain, set a clear goal and date, document, follow policy if it doesn't improve.)
- Someone ignores a safety rule. (Stop it right away, correct it privately, document it, follow up. Safety is not negotiable.)
- How do you handle a call-out that leaves you short? (Re-balance the floor by skill, cross-train ahead of time, tell your manager early.)
- How do you give feedback someone doesn't want to hear?
- How do you hit numbers? Which KPIs matter here? (Know yours: units per hour, accuracy, on-time ship, safety incidents, attendance.)
- Tell me about a mistake you made and what you learned.
- Where do you see yourself in 3 to 5 years?

## 4. Your edge (say it plainly)
- You already train people, here and at other sites. The company trusted you to represent this DC.
- You're consistent and rules-based: show up, follow the process, review what went wrong, fix it.
- You lead by example on the floor: phones away, safety first.

## 5. Questions to ask them (pick 2 or 3)
- What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days?
- What's the biggest challenge the shift has right now?
- Which numbers will I be measured on, and how often are they reviewed?
- What training or support do new supervisors get?
- What made past supervisors here successful?

## 6. Day before / day of
- Know your numbers: your rate, accuracy, how many people you've trained, which sites you traveled to.
- Know the company's safety and attendance policies well enough to quote them.
- Clean, a step above floor clothes unless told otherwise. Arrive 10 minutes early.
- Bring a notepad with your 5 stories as bullet points and your 3 questions.
- Firm handshake, eye contact, slow down when you answer. Pause before answering is fine.
- Thank-you note or message to the interviewer the same day: 2 sentences, one specific thing from the talk.

## 7. Don'ts
- Don't trash a current supervisor or coworker.
- Don't bring up side businesses or trading.
- Don't say "I'll be the cool supervisor." Say "fair and consistent."
- Don't guess numbers. "I don't have the exact number, but it was around X" is fine.
