## Frame-off restoration (Tim, 10/20/2026)

**What frame-off means on this car.** The 1978-81 Cutlass Supreme is the downsized A-body
(renamed G-body in 1982; same platform as 1978-88 Monte Carlo, Regal, Grand Prix, Malibu, El
Camino). It is body-on-frame: a separate steel perimeter frame, with the body bolted to it
through rubber body mount bushings at about a dozen points. Frame-off means the body comes off
the frame completely. Both get stripped to bare metal, repaired and coated, every chassis part
gets rebuilt on the bare frame, and the body goes back on with new mount bushings. It is the
right call here: the rust is in the roof, C-pillars and (likely) rear window channel, the car
has been sitting in dirt, the LS twin turbo needs new everything underneath anyway, and the
panoramic roof is a body structure job that is easiest with the body off and stripped.

**What it fixes from the 10/15 videos:** vinyl top rust (top comes off, body gets blasted to
bare metal), both C-pillar quarters (driver worst) and the rear window channel (cut out and
replaced on the bare shell), the algae/leak (new rear window gasket at reassembly), the cowl full
of leaves (cowl and drains cleaned and repaired when blasted), and the sitting-in-dirt damage to
the underside. Still unseen: frame, floors, trunk pan and engine bay. Do the 10-minute
flashlight check below first so the panel order is right; frame-off covers them either way, but
a rotted frame changes the plan (a clean used G-body frame runs about $500-1,500 in NC and
can beat repairing a bad one).

**DIY vs shop.** Tim can do: documenting, teardown, drivetrain pull, body lift, chassis rebuild,
brake and fuel lines, body mounts, drivetrain install and reassembly. That is most of the hours.
Shop work: media blasting (or dipping), frame powder coat, the panoramic roof, and paint. Body
rust repair and frame boxing go either way: DIY if Tim learns to MIG weld (a weekend class at a
community college like Piedmont CC or Durham Tech helps), shop if not. Shop metal work is the
biggest swing in the budget.

**Space and tools.** The car will be apart for 1-3 years of weekends.
- Space: a 2-car garage (about 20x20 minimum, 24x24 is better) with power and lighting. The
  body on a dolly and the bare frame each take a car-size footprint, plus room for parts shelves.
  If Tim has no garage, a rented garage unit in Person County runs about $100-250 a month (not in
  the totals).
- Engine hoist (2-ton folding) and an engine stand: $300-500.
- Body dolly (build one from square tube and casters, or buy) $150-500, or a rotisserie
  (2,000-3,000 lb, about $900-2,600) that lets the shell turn on its side for floor and trunk work.
- Jack stands, floor jack, impact gun, penetrating oil, cutoff wheel and grinder: $200-500 if not
  already owned.
- For DIY metal work: 120/240V MIG welder, welding helmet, clamps, spot weld drill bits:
  $400-900 (this is the low end of the welding line).
- Tools line in the budget: $600-1,500 (hoist, stand, dolly, stands, bags). Rotisserie is extra.

**Prices** are 2026 parts listings and forum quotes, rounded: G-body body mount kits $100-230
(gbodyforum shop, Rubber the Right Way); 1981-88 Cutlass quarter pair about $350-410 and lower
quarter patches about $120 a pair (Restoparts, OPGI; those list 1981-88, so confirm the part fits

OER; rotisseries $900-2,600 (JEGS, Atlas). Blast and powder coat numbers are older forum quotes
raised for 2026; get two local quotes (Durham, Greensboro, Danville VA) before booking.

## Condition from videos, 10/15/2026
Three 30-second clips (VID_20261015_123009, 123015, 123019; copies in Drive folder
Cutlass `1qaY4MnwH_7qRSq5yQ8G1QmbBPJjF4L9R`). Outside walkaround only. Not shown: engine
bay, under the car, frame, floors, trunk inside, dash.

**Body style:** 2-door coupe (Tim, 10/15), white with a black vinyl top. Coupe parts are
easy to find for these cars.

**Paint and panels:** white paint is oxidized and covered in mold and lichen spots, worst
on the hood, trunk lid and rear panel. Hood, trunk lid, fenders and doors look straight
from the video; no big dents seen. Chrome bumpers, grilles and trim are all there.

**Rust (seen):**
- Vinyl top is cracked and peeling. Where it has lifted (passenger side roof near the
  windshield, and along the rear of the roof) the metal underneath has surface rust.
  Water has been sitting under the vinyl, so expect more rust under the parts still stuck down.
- Driver side C-pillar / rear quarter: heavy rust bleeding down the quarter from the base
  of the vinyl and the quarter window corner. Some bubbling at the seam. This is the
  worst spot seen.
- Passenger side C-pillar: a rust run down the quarter at the same spot, lighter.
- Rear window: green algae on the inside of the glass, and the trim channel is packed with
  debris. That points to a leaking rear window seal, which on these cars usually means
  rust in the rear window channel, package tray and trunk floor. Check all three.
- Cowl (base of the windshield) is packed with leaves. Clogged cowl drains send water to
  the floors, so check the front floor pans.

**Glass and lights:** driver side quarter window (behind the door) is shattered (crazed but still in the
frame). Windshield intact with leaves on the wipers. One headlight opening on the front
is empty.

**Interior (through the glass):** blue cloth seats with striped seat covers, a broom and
loose junk inside, heavy dust. Expect mildew given the rear window leak.

**Tires and stance:** tires are flat and the car is sinking into the dirt, with whitewalls
on wire wheel covers. Sitting on the ground keeps the floors and frame wet.

**Audio:** nothing about the car; family talking ("Look at this car, I don't know how you
don't think you're gonna fix this").

**Verdict:** a solid-looking body from the outside with the usual 1978-81 A-body vinyl top
problem. The rust that's visible is fixable at home with patch panels. The real unknowns
are the rear window channel, trunk floor, floors and frame, and none of those were on video.
