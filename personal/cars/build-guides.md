# Build guides: all four vehicles (10/21/2026)

Tim asked (10/21): a step-by-step guide for each build, in the order that gives the cleanest,
lowest-hassle build, plus the biggest engine he could build for each one if he builds his own.

Each phase lists: **Do** (the work), **Have on hand** (buy before you start the phase, so the
car never sits apart waiting on parts), **DIY / shop**, **Mistakes to avoid**, **Checkpoint**
(do not start the next phase until it passes) and **Time**. Times assume weekends only: one
weekend is about 12-16 shop hours. Shop lead times (machine shop, blasting, chrome, paint) run
in parallel with other work, so book them early.

Prices and parts come from [parts-lists.md](parts-lists.md) and [build-plan.md](build-plan.md)
unless marked "checked 10/21". Engine prices are retailer listings found 10/21; confirm before
buying.

Google Docs (one per vehicle folder under Cars):
[Cutlass](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1LLS2XLBkq5OxGyhhWxxvSQHiG-ZyAc2UtBGJ0FkDcxQ/edit),
[Charger](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rfL7pRb0ehIUNtlBOHbSZsuJ7Ku2VpWemuoGlX_v6jQ/edit),

## Rules for every build

1. **Baseline before buying.** Test and measure what you have (compression, leak-down,
   charging, frame) before spending on power parts.
2. **One car apart at a time.** Cruze back together and running first, then the Charger, then
   the Cutlass. The Busa drips get fixed now because they are small.
3. **Parts on the shelf before the wrench comes out.** Every phase lists what to have on hand.
4. **Mechanical and safety before power, power before looks.** Brakes and oiling go in before
   boost.
5. **All cutting, welding and drilling before paint, wrap or powder coat.** Then test-fit
   everything (lights, trim, glass, wheels, aero, pods) before final paint.
6. **Wiring planned before interior.** Draw the diagram, drill the pass-throughs, run the wires,
   then put the interior in.
7. **Tune last, on a finished car.** Then break-in and a shakedown (about 500 miles) before full
   power.
8. **Bag, tag, photograph and log.** Photos of every harness and bracket before removal, labeled
   bags, and every torque value and measurement written in the car's maintenance log.

## Corrections found while writing this (10/21)

- **Cruze engine block is cast iron, not aluminum.** GM describes the 1.4T (LUJ/LUV) block as
  gray cast iron with an aluminum head. The deck cleaning plan in the Cruze file still applies
  (no abrasive discs; the grit is the problem either way).
- **Cruze 1.4T likely uses a timing chain, not a belt.** ZZP's 1.4L forged long block lists a
  "new timing chain kit". The parts-list line "timing belt kit" should probably be a timing chain
  kit (chain, guides, tensioner). Confirm against the engine code from the VIN before ordering.
- **Charger transmission:** a Dodge announcement says the Pursuit's 5.7 RWD got the TorqueFlite
  8-speed (8HP70); the 5-speed W5A580 listings found are for older (2006-2014) cars. No 2016
  Pursuit spec sheet confirmed it. Quick check: the 5-speed has a transmission fill tube at the
  back of the engine; the 8HP70 has no fill tube (fill plug on the case). Or pull the build sheet
  by VIN.

## 1979 Oldsmobile Cutlass Supreme (frame-off, LS twin turbo)

**The build:** 1979 G-body coupe, frame-off. Forged LS with twin turbos under the hood (front
corners of the bay), built 4L80E automatic with a B&M Pro Stick console shifter, Ridetech
coilovers and StrongArms (no air ride), mini-tubs, Currie 9 inch, custom panoramic glass roof,
shaved and flush body, tucked bumpers, LED tail panel, dark pewter paint, 3 inch stainless
duals with switchable electric cutouts, burgundy leather into the trunk, Holley Terminator X
Max with the 12.3 dash, RestoMod Air Vapir 3-S A/C.

**Total time:** about 1.5-3 years of weekends. Shop lead times (blasting, roof, machine shop,
paint) set the pace, so book them early and work on other phases while you wait.

**The key idea:** build the whole car once in bare metal (mock-up), weld every bracket and cut
every hole, then take it apart for coating and paint, then build it the final time. Doing this
in order is what keeps you from grinding through fresh powder coat or paint.

### Step by step

**1. Plan, inspect and freeze the design (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: the 10-minute flashlight check in the Cutlass file (trunk floor, floors, rear window
  channel, frame rails ahead of the rear wheels, body mounts). Find the VIN and get the title
  clean. Write down the final specs: wheel and tire sizes, ride height, tub size, turbo layout,
  exhaust route, dash, console and shifter location. Get quotes for blasting, the panoramic
  roof, metal work, paint and the trim shop.
- Have on hand: flashlight, camera, notebook; garage space (20x20 minimum) with power and light.
- DIY / shop: DIY.
- Mistakes: buying engine or appearance parts before the frame and floors are inspected. If the
  frame is rotted, a clean used G-body frame ($500-1,500) beats repairing it.
- Checkpoint: written spec sheet and a budget for phase 1. Frame verdict: repair or replace.

**2. Teardown and document (3-6 weekends)**
- Do: photograph everything, bag and tag every bolt by area, paint-pen mark brackets. Strip the
  interior, glass, trim, bumpers, wiring and vinyl top. Pull the old drivetrain and the 7.5 rear
  end.
- Have on hand: zip bags, tags, bins, shelves, engine hoist and stand, penetrating oil, impact gun,
  cutoff tool, jack stands ($600-1,500 tools line).
- DIY / shop: DIY.
- Mistakes: skipping the photos. In two years nobody remembers where a bracket went.
- Checkpoint: car is a bare body on the frame; every part is labeled and shelved.

**3. Body off the frame (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: before unbolting, weld or bolt temporary braces across both door openings (X-brace) so the
  shell cannot sag. Measure and record body reference points (door openings corner to corner,
  diagonals). Cut and replace rusted body mount bolts. Lift the body with 4 people or the hoist,
  set it on a dolly or rotisserie.
- Have on hand: square tube for door braces, body dolly or rotisserie, body mount bushing kit
  (for later), new bolts and cage nut repair parts.
- DIY / shop: DIY.
- Mistakes: lifting without door braces. A cut-roof car with rust in the C-pillars can bend and
  the doors will never fit again.
- Checkpoint: body measurements recorded; frame rolls free.

**4. Blast and inspect (shop, 2-6 weeks lead time; 1 weekend of hauling)**
- Do: media blast the shell inside and out and get epoxy primer on it the same week. Blast the
  frame bare, then wipe it with a rust preventive (or shoot weld-through primer) while it waits.
  Do not powder coat the frame yet.
- Have on hand: trailer or transport, epoxy primer if the shop does not supply it.
- DIY / shop: shop.
- Mistakes: leaving bare steel unprimed (it flash rusts in NC humidity within days); powder
  coating the frame now (every bracket in step 6 would burn it).
- Checkpoint: rust map of the bare shell and frame. Update the panel order.

**5. Frame repair and square check (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: repair rot (rear rails ahead of the rear wheels, kick-ups, steering box area), box the
  frame, add the steering box brace. Then check square.
- Have on hand: MIG welder or a shop booked, boxing plates, steering box brace, tape measure,
  plumb bob, GM G-body frame dimension chart.
- DIY / shop: DIY if Tim welds; otherwise shop.
- Mistakes: welding one long bead down one side (pulls the frame). Stitch weld and alternate
  sides.
- **Checkpoint: frame square.** Set the frame on level stands, drop a plumb bob to the floor at
  matching points, and measure the X diagonals front, middle and rear. Diagonals within 1/8 inch
  (3 mm) of each other. Fix it now; nothing after this step can fix a crooked frame.

**6. Rolling chassis mock-up on the bare frame (4-8 weekends)**
This is the most important step for a clean build. Everything bolts on in bare metal.
- Do:
  - Ridetech coilovers and StrongArms, the Currie 9 inch, steering box, and mock-up wheels and
    tires. Set the ride height.
  - LS (the junkyard core works as the mock-up block before it goes to the machine shop) on the
    Hooker mounts, 4L80E (or an empty case) on the Hooker crossmember, Holley 302-2 pan.
  - Check oil pan to steering linkage and oil pan to ground clearance.
  - Set the body on the frame (new mount bushings can wait; use the old ones) to check tires
    against the quarters and decide the mini-tub size.
  - Front clip on: place the turbos, hot pipes, wastegates, intercooler, radiator and fans,
    steering shaft path, RestoMod Air condenser and line route, battery tray delete.
  - Tack the exhaust: downpipes, cutout Y-pipes, mid-pipes, X-pipe, mufflers, tailpipes, tips.
  - Mark and weld every tab to the frame: turbo and intercooler mounts, exhaust hangers, brake
    and fuel line clips, battery cable clamps, cutout harness clips, tub frame notch.
- Have on hand: mock-up wheels/tires or a wheel fitment tool, coilover kit, rear end, engine
  core, trans or case, mounts, crossmember, pan, turbo kit parts, exhaust tubing and bends,
  angle finder, a strap to compress the suspension.
- DIY / shop: DIY for the chassis and placement; shop for hot side and exhaust fab (10-25 hrs).
- Mistakes: ordering billet wheels with custom backspacing before this step; cutting tubs
  before wheels are at ride height; routing brake and fuel lines on the same side of the rail
  as a dump outlet.
- **Checkpoints:**
  - Tire clearance at full bump (spring off, cycle the suspension) and at full lock, front and
    rear, with the body on.
  - Pipes and pan above the bottom of the frame rails; 3.5-4 inch clearance at ride height.
  - Engine and trans angle about 2-3 degrees down at the rear; pinion angle set so driveline
    working angles match within about 0.5 degree and stay under 3 degrees. Driveshaft gets
    measured only after this.
  - Turbo oil drains run downhill to the pan with no sags.
  - Hood closes over the intake and cold side.

**7. Body metal work on the bare shell (shop or DIY; 2-6 months)**
- Do, in this order: rust repair (quarters, rear window channel, package tray, trunk pan,
  floors, cowl), panoramic roof framing and reinforcement plates, shave drip rails with the roof
  job, shave emblems, antenna and marker holes, door handle shave with poppers, mini-tubs, tail
  panel opening, rear valance tip cutouts, rocker heat shield mounts for the dumps, bumper
  bracket narrowing, smooth the firewall and inner fenders (or tubular inner fenders) for the
  turbos.
- Then drill every hole the finished car needs: A/C bulkhead hoses, hidden harness pass-throughs,
  shifter cable, console mounts, fuel pump wiring to the trunk, battery cable path, CO detector.
- Have on hand: repro panels (OPGI; confirm 1979 fit), roof glass spec, AutoLoc popper kit,
  RestoMod Air kit in hand (to place the firewall holes), B&M shifter and console plan.
- DIY / shop: roof is shop; the rest is DIY if Tim welds, otherwise shop.
- Mistakes: cutting the roof without the body braced and measured; drilling firewall holes after
  paint.
- Checkpoint: re-measure the body reference points from step 3 (same within 1/8 inch). Doors,
  hood and trunk hang and close with even gaps.

**8. Full test-fit before paint (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: hang and fit everything: glass (flush windshield, rear, roof panel), bumpers, LED tail
  panel, headlights (confirm 7x6 from the old lens number), parking lamps, poppers, tips, dump
  outlets, RestoMod Air under-dash unit, console and shifter, Holley 12.3 dash panel, seats.
  Fix what does not fit, then take it all off for paint.
- Have on hand: all exterior and fit-critical interior parts.
- DIY / shop: DIY (glass shop for the roof glass check).
- Mistakes: skipping this. Every bracket that needs grinding after paint means a touch-up.
- Checkpoint: every part fits with no cutting or drilling left.

**9. Engine build (machine shop runs in parallel; 2-4 weekends of assembly)**
- Do: send the block for crack check (magnaflux) and sonic test before any machine work. Machine
  shop picks the final bore, then order pistons. Bore, hone with a torque plate, deck, line
  hone, balance. Assemble with ARP head studs and MLS gaskets.
- Have on hand: forged rotating assembly (ordered after the bore is set), ARP studs, gaskets,
  cam and springs, Melling pump, timing set, assembly lube, micrometers or have the shop measure.
- DIY / shop: machine work is shop; assembly DIY with the shop's measurements, or shop.
- Mistakes: buying pistons before the bore is final; using the naturally aspirated ring gap
  (boost needs a wider top ring gap; use the piston maker's boost chart).
- Checkpoint: build sheet with bearing clearances, ring gaps, piston-to-valve clearance and cam
  degreed. Keep it in the Cutlass folder.

**10. Coatings and paint (shop, 1-3 months)**
- Do: powder coat the frame and all brackets; ceramic coat the hot pipes, turbine housings,
  downpipes and cutout Y-pipes; paint the bay, jambs and body (dark pewter). Ask the paint shop
  whether to paint with the body off (on the dolly) or on the finished chassis.
- Have on hand: paint supplies (PPG or Axalta), the deposit, transport.
- DIY / shop: shop.
- Mistakes: rushing reassembly on soft paint; scratching panels during assembly. Tape edges and
  use fender covers.
- Checkpoint: paint cured per the shop before glass and trim go on.

**11. Final chassis, then body drop (3-6 weekends)**
- Do: on the coated frame: suspension, rear end, brakes, stainless hard lines, fuel tank, pump
  and lines. New body mount bushings, body down. Engine and 4L80E in, turbos, intercooler,
  cooling, oil feeds and drains, exhaust final weld and hangers, cutouts and their relay
  wiring, heat shields.
- Have on hand: body mount kit, all brake and fuel parts, AN fittings and lines, coolant, fluids,
  heat sleeve and shields.
- DIY / shop: DIY; exhaust final weld at the shop.
- Checkpoints: brake system bled and pressure checked for leaks; fuel system pressure and leak
  check (key on) before the engine runs; every AN fitting torqued and marked.

**12. Wiring (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: one diagram first, then install: Painless or American Autowire chassis harness, Holley
  Terminator X Max, 12.3 dash, trunk battery with cutoff and 1/0 cable, fuel pump relay, cutout
  relays and console toggle with indicator light, RestoMod Air ECU, PRNDL/neutral safety from
  the 4L80E, LED lights and tail panel, poppers. Harness runs hidden in the fenders and under
  the cowl.
- Have on hand: harness kits, relays, fuses, heat sleeve, grommets, crimpers, labels.
- DIY / shop: DIY.
- Mistakes: putting carpet or panels in before the wiring is tested.
- Checkpoint: every circuit tested key-on with the engine off; nothing hot, no blown fuses.

**13. First start and break-in (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: prime the oil system with the plugs out until there is pressure; fill each turbo through
  the feed line before first crank. Key on and check fuel pressure and leaks. Start on a base
  map, check timing, oil pressure, leaks, coolant. Heat cycle, then drive on wastegate spring
  pressure only (no added boost) for about 500 miles to seat the rings. Change the oil after
  the first 50-100 miles and again at 500.
- Have on hand: oil and filters, coolant, laptop with Holley software, fire extinguisher,
  CO detector in the cabin.
- Mistakes: raising boost before break-in; idling with the cutouts open.
- Checkpoint: after 500 miles, compression and leak-down on all 8 cylinders (within 10% of each
  other; under about 10% leak-down).

**14. Interior and trunk (shop plus DIY; 1-3 months)**
- Do: sound deadening and heat barrier (floor over the X-pipe and mufflers), burgundy carpet,
  seats, door panels, headliner, console with the B&M shifter plate, trunk panels hiding the
  battery and fuel wiring. Leave the trunk and kick panels easy to remove.
- DIY / shop: trim shop for the leather; DIY for the rest.
- Checkpoint: A/C charged and cold; every switch works.

**15. Dyno tune, alignment and shakedown (2-3 weekends plus a dyno day)**
- Do: dyno tune on the Terminator X: fuel, timing, boost in steps, 4L80E shift and lockup
  tables. Four-wheel alignment and corner balance. Then 500 miles of easy driving and recheck
  every fitting, bolt and fluid.
- Have on hand: $600-1,200 for the dyno tune, alignment.
- Checkpoint: no leaks, no codes, temps stable in summer traffic, brakes pull straight.

### Don't do this before that (Cutlass)

- Don't powder coat the frame before every tab and bracket is welded (turbo mounts, hangers,
  line clips, tub notch).
- Don't paint before the roof, shaving, tubs, tail panel, inner fenders, firewall holes, tip and
  dump openings and the full test-fit are done.
- Don't lift the body or cut the roof before the door openings are braced and the body is
  measured.
- Don't cut tubs before the wheels and tires are at ride height with the body on.
- Don't order billet wheels before the backspacing is measured at ride height.
- Don't order the driveshaft before the engine, trans, rear end and ride height are final.
- Don't buy pistons before the machine shop sets the bore.
- Don't order the shifter cable before the console and shifter are mocked up.
- Don't ceramic coat the hot side before fab and fit are final.
- Don't install carpet or panels before the wiring and A/C lines are done and tested.
- Don't add boost past the wastegate spring before the 500-mile break-in and leak-down.

### Biggest engine for the Cutlass (LS)

Cubic inches = bore x bore x stroke x 6.2832. All LS blocks share the same outside size at the
standard 9.240 inch deck, so a 6.0 and a 454 fit the G-body bay the same. Tall-deck blocks are
wider and taller.

| Block | Bore x stroke (in) | Size | Notes | Block price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.3 iron (LM7/LMG) | 3.78 x 3.622 | 325 ci, 5.3 L | Small bore; with a 4.000 crank about 359 ci (5.9 L) | $500-1,200 junkyard |
| 6.2 aluminum (L92/LS3) | 4.065 x 3.622 | 376 ci, 6.2 L | Light; fine to about 700-800 hp with studs; 4.000 crank gives 415 ci | $2,000-4,000 used engine |
| **6.0 iron (LQ4/LQ9/LY6)** | 4.000 x 3.622 | 364 ci, 6.0 L | Proven to 1,000+ hp with forged parts; the parts-list plan | $800-2,000 junkyard |
| 6.0 iron with forged 4.000 stroker | 4.000 (or 4.030 over) x 4.000 | 402-408 ci, 6.6-6.7 L | Common kit sizes; same block | rotating assembly $2,200-3,800 |
| Dart LS Next iron | 4.125 x 4.000 (bores to 4.200; Dart's suggested max stroke 4.100) | 427 ci, 7.0 L (up to about 454) | Thick deck and walls for boost; 9.240 deck | about $4,240 listed (Dartheads, 4.000 bore) |
| Chevrolet Performance LSX454 block 19417353 | 4.185 x 4.125 | 454 ci, 7.4 L | Biggest on the standard deck | $5,013-5,872 listed (BTR, JEGS) |
| Chevrolet Performance LSX tall deck 19417354 | 4.185 x 4.500 max | 495 ci, 8.1 L | 9.720 deck: wider and taller, needs custom headers and hot side, hood clearance; does not suit front-corner twins in a G-body | $4,325 listed (SDPC) |

**Biggest:** about 495 ci (8.1 L) on a tall-deck LSX. **Biggest that fits sensibly with
under-hood twins:** 454 ci (7.4 L) on a standard-deck LSX454 or Dart block.

**Recommendation: a junkyard 6.0 iron block with a forged 4.000 stroker, 402-408 ci
(6.6-6.7 L).** It keeps the parts-list budget, the block is proven past the 800-1,000 hp
target, more cubic inches spool the twins sooner and make the same power at lower boost, and
the stock-size block keeps the turbo layout simple. Move to a Dart LS Next iron 427 if the
junkyard block fails the crack or sonic check, or if the goal goes past about 1,100 hp. The
built 4L80E (rated by builders around 800-1,000 hp) is the next limit, not the engine.

## 2016 Dodge Charger Pursuit (5.7 HEMI, stays RWD, ProCharger)

**The build:** 188k miles, RWD, ProCharger HO intercooled, widebody (Clinched Flares or Vicrez),
white pearl paint, dark bronze concave wheels, Brembo 6-piston swap, 230 mm LSD rear, axles and
driveshaft, cradle and suspension bushings, camber and toe arms, coilovers, quad-tip cat-back,
black and bronze interior, trunk audio. Oil change past due.

**Total time:** about 6-12 months of weekends, mostly waiting on paint and wheels. The car stays
drivable between phases except during paint.

### Step by step

**1. Baseline and catch up (1 weekend)**
- Do: oil change now (7 qt 5W-20, MO-339). Scan for codes. Check engine hours in the cluster if
  shown (police cars idle for hours; heavy idle time wears the engine like extra miles). Listen
  for the HEMI lifter tick. Check trans fluid condition and confirm which transmission it has
  (see Corrections above). Check for the factory engine oil cooler. Pull the VIN build sheet.
- Then the most important test: **compression and leak-down** on all 8 cylinders, engine warm.
- Have on hand: oil and filter, compression tester and leak-down tester (or $0-150 at a shop),
  scan tool.
- Checkpoint: cylinders within 10% of each other on compression; leak-down under about 10% is
  good, 10-20% watch it, over 20% fix before boost. If weak: plan a built engine (see the
  engine section) before buying the supercharger.

**2. Maintenance and repairs (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: water pump, thermostat, hoses, 16 plugs, trans service (fluid and pan filter). If the
  lifters tick: MDS-delete lifters and a cam that works with a ProCharger, done once.
- Have on hand: Mopar water pump and thermostat, hoses, NGK plugs, ZF fluid and filter pan.
- DIY / shop: DIY; lifters and cam can go to a shop.
- Mistakes: picking a cam without asking ProCharger or the tuner what works under boost.
- Checkpoint: 300 miles with no leaks, no codes, steady temps.

**3. Stop before go: brakes and oiling (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: used SRT/Hellcat 6-piston Brembo front and 4-piston rear, braided lines, fresh fluid.
  Catch cans and oil cooler (skip the cooler if the factory one is there), AN lines.
- Have on hand: complete brake set with brackets, rotors, pads, lines, DOT 4 fluid.
- Mistakes: buying wheels that do not clear the Brembos (20 inch clears them).
- Checkpoint: firm pedal, straight stops, no leaks.

**4. Drivetrain and chassis (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: 230 mm LSD rear, cradle bushing inserts, front tension struts, rear camber and toe arms,
  end links, strut brace, sway bars, coilovers. Do all the bushings in one pass. Axles and a
  one-piece driveshaft now if the plan goes past about 650 hp.
- Have on hand: all suspension parts in one order, diff fluid, torque wrench.
- Mistakes: setting the final ride height now. Leave the coilovers near stock height until the
  widebody and wheels are on.
- Checkpoint: temporary alignment; no clunks.

**5. Power (2-3 weekends plus a dyno day)**
- Do: unlock the PCM (2015+ Mopar PCMs must be unlocked before HP Tuners), install the ProCharger
  complete kit (fuel parts included), base tune from the kit, transmission tune. Then the dyno
  tune after about 200 miles.
- Have on hand: the complete kit, 91-93 octane, the dyno appointment.
- Mistakes: running boost on the old fuel pump or without the tune; skipping the 8HP70 tune
  (line pressure).
- Checkpoint: datalogs show safe air-fuel ratio, no knock retard, intake air temps under
  control. Repeat the leak-down after 500 miles.

**6. Exhaust (1 weekend)**
- Do: quad-tip cat-back (Borla ATAK or Corsa Xtreme). Hang it now so the diffuser in step 7 is
  fitted around the real tip location.

**7. Widebody and aero test fit (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: set the ride height you plan to run, then mount the flares with the final wheels and tires
  (or same-size mock-ups) on the car. Check tire-to-flare clearance at full lock and with the
  suspension compressed. Fit the splitter, skirts, diffuser and ducktail. Mark and drill every
  hole. Fill the Pursuit spotlight, antenna and push bar holes. Then remove the kit for paint.
- Have on hand: the widebody kit, aero, wheels (order after measuring the flares), body filler
  and weld-in plugs.
- DIY / shop: test fit DIY; filling holes and kit bodywork at the shop.
- Mistakes: ordering wheels before the flares are on; drilling after paint.
- Checkpoint: every panel fits with even gaps and no rubbing.

**8. Paint (shop, 4-8 weeks)**
- Do: white pearl tri-coat on the whole car and kit, holes filled. Black grille and badges.
- Mistakes: painting only the kit (tri-coat white is hard to match panel to panel).

**9. Lights, final assembly and ride height (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: LED headlights (check the Pursuit halogen harness) go in while the bumper is off. Set the
  final ride height, then a performance alignment.
- Checkpoint: alignment printout in spec; headlights aimed.

**10. Interior and trunk audio (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: plan the audio wiring first (power wire from the battery, fuse at the battery, ground,
  remote turn-on). Pull the police equipment wiring leftovers. Black and bronze interior, then
  the trunk build.
- Checkpoint: charging voltage stays above about 13.5 V with the audio up.

**11. Final dyno check and shakedown**
- Recheck the tune with the final exhaust and tires; 500 miles; recheck bolts and fluids.

### Don't do this before that (Charger)

- Don't buy the supercharger before compression and leak-down pass.
- Don't add boost before the brakes, LSD and fuel parts are in and the PCM is unlocked.
- Don't buy wheels before the flares are mounted and measured.
- Don't set the final ride height or alignment before the widebody and wheels are on.
- Don't paint before every kit hole is drilled and every Pursuit hole is filled.
- Don't launch hard past about 650 hp on the stock axles and two-piece driveshaft.
- Don't put the interior back before the audio power and signal wires are run.

### Biggest engine for the Charger (Gen III HEMI)

| Engine | Bore x stroke (in) | Size | Notes | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stock 5.7 Eagle** | 3.917 x 3.578 | 345 ci, 5.7 L | Overbore limited to about 0.020 in (thin walls) | in the car |
| 5.7 stroker | 3.937 x 4.050 | about 394 ci, 6.5 L | Forged crank and pistons in the 5.7 block | $3,500-6,000 rotating assembly plus machine work (estimate) |
| 6.2 Hellcat | 4.090 x 3.580 | 376 ci, 6.2 L | Factory supercharged; needs 8HP90, wiring, cooling; too much swap for this car | crate prices vary widely; confirm |
| **6.4 / 392** | 4.090 x 3.720 | 392 ci, 6.4 L | Same outside size as the 5.7; bolts to the same trans | Mopar P5160617AA 392 long block $6,995 listed (SDPC); used bare block about $1,140 |
| 426 stroker (392 block) | 4.090 x 4.050 | 426 ci, 7.0 L | Biggest common build | American Powertrain 426 long block $14,299 listed; Edelbrock 426 $24,367 listed |
| Dart Iron Eagle Gen III block | up to 4.250 bore | about 460 ci (4.050 stroke), up to about 480 ci if Dart allows a 4.250 stroke (not confirmed) | Race-level block; Dart claims 2,000+ hp | confirm price with Dart |

**Biggest:** about 460-480 ci on a Dart Iron Eagle block. **Biggest common:** 426 ci (7.0 L).

**What limits it first is the drivetrain, not the engine.** The 8HP70 holds roughly 600-650 hp
at the wheels with a tune (rough community figure; confirm with the trans builder); past that it
needs an 8HP90 swap or a built unit. The stock axles and driveshaft are the next limit.

**Recommendation: keep the 5.7 if it passes compression and leak-down, and run the ProCharger
at the kit's boost.** That is the cheapest, most reliable path to the goal. If the engine tests
weak at 188k, replace it with a **6.4 / 392** (forged pistons preferred under boost), which bolts
in, adds 47 ci and stays inside what the 8HP70 can take. Confirm with ProCharger which kit and
tune fit a 392 swap in a 5.7 car before buying. A 426 only makes sense with the 8HP90 and the
axles and driveshaft already done.

## 2012 Chevy Cruze LT RS (1.4T, head off)

**The build:** white car going gloss black wrap (3M 2080 or Avery SW900), burnt orange interior
accents, smoked LED taillights, LED halo projector headlights, Air Lift 3P/3H air ride, 18 inch
gloss black wheels, lip, skirts and diffuser, single tip, black bowties. Upgraded turbo (ZZP
Z04) only if the engine tests healthy. 182,995 miles; engine code LUJ or LUV (confirm by VIN,
8th digit).

**Total time:** about 4-8 months of weekends. Getting it running is 3-6 weekends.

### Step by step

**1. Finish the head job right (2-4 weekends plus 1-2 weeks at the machine shop)**
- Do: find out why the head came off and write it in the Cruze file. Look at each piston top
  and ring land for cracks (the 1.4T is known for cracked pistons from detonation) and the
  cylinder walls for scoring. Clean the deck per the Cruze file. Machine shop checks the head for
  warp and leak-tests the valves. Replace the timing parts (chain kit; see Corrections above), water
  pump, thermostat housing and the valve cover with the PCV. New TTY head bolts, new gasket
  installed dry.
- Have on hand: gasket set, TTY bolts, timing kit, water pump, thermostat housing, valve cover,
  plugs, coolant (Dex-Cool), dexos oil, torque wrench and angle gauge.
- Mistakes: abrasive discs on the deck; reusing head bolts; skipping the timing alignment tools.
- **Checkpoint:** if a piston is cracked or a wall is scored, stop: the bottom end needs work
  (see the engine section) before the car goes back together.

**2. First start and proof run (2 weekends)**
- Do: start, check leaks, bleed the cooling system. Drive 500-1,000 miles normally. Mark the
  dipstick and track oil use.
- **Checkpoint:** compression and leak-down on all 4 cylinders (within 10%; under about 10%
  leak-down), cooling system pressure test, no coolant in the oil, oil use under about 1 quart
  per 2,000 miles.

**3. Reliability and safety (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: check the turbo oil feed and return lines (known leakers), coils, plugs gapped for boost.
  Brakes: check if the rear is drum or disc; braided lines and the front brake upgrade. Catch can
  and oil cooler.
- Use a dexos1 Gen 2 or newer oil: it is formulated against low-speed pre-ignition, which is
  what cracks 1.4T pistons.

**4. Turbo decision gate**
- **Pass** (healthy tests, no oil use): ZZP Z04 turbo, downpipe, intercooler, intake, fuel
  parts, then the tune (Trifecta or ZZP). Dyno or datalog tune after install.
- **Fail:** keep the stock turbo and skip the power phase, or put in a forged long block first
  (engine section). Do not put a bigger turbo on a tired 183k engine with cast pistons.

**5. Air ride and wheels (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: struts and bags, Air Lift 3P (or 3H with height sensors), tank and compressor in the
  trunk, lines routed away from the exhaust and sharp edges. Plan the controller and switch
  wiring now. Mount the wheels (5x105 or adapters), air the car out, check fender lip and
  tire clearance, roll the fenders if needed, then buy the stretched tires and set camber.
- Mistakes: buying tires before the aired-out fit check; rolling fenders after the wrap (cracks
  the film).
- Checkpoint: soapy water leak test on every fitting; car holds pressure overnight; alignment.

**6. Exterior parts on the white car (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: LED halo headlights and smoked taillights (SW-0126A-BRB; check the listing says sedan),
  front lip, side skirts, diffuser, single tip exhaust, remove the chrome bowties. Drill every
  hole now.
- Checkpoint: everything mounted; the car looks finished except the color.

**7. Gloss black wrap (shop, 3-5 days)**
- Do: wrap last on the outside, after every part is drilled and mounted. Ask the shop to remove
  lights and handles for tucked edges; add door jambs if budget allows (about $500 more).
  Decide whether the lip, skirts and diffuser get wrapped or stay their own black.
- Mistakes: wrapping before parts are mounted (cutting film later lifts the edges); waxing or
  pressure washing the edges in the first week.

**8. Burnt orange interior (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: run the ambient LED and air management wiring first, then the seat covers or upholstery
  with burnt orange stitching, steering wheel stitching and trim accents.

**9. Final tune and alignment**
- Recheck the tune at final ride height and with the final exhaust; alignment at the height you
  drive at.

### Don't do this before that (Cruze)

- Don't put the engine back together before checking the pistons and walls.
- Don't buy the bigger turbo before the post-rebuild tests and the oil-use check pass.
- Don't buy stretched tires before the wheels are test-fit aired out.
- Don't wrap before the lights, aero, exhaust and fender rolling are done.
- Don't install interior trim before the air ride controller and ambient lighting wires are run.
- Don't reuse the head bolts or use abrasive pads on the deck.

### Biggest engine for the Cruze

| Engine | Bore x stroke (mm) | Size | Notes | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stock 1.4T (LUJ/LUV)** | 72.5 x 82.6 | 1,364 cc, 83 ci | Iron block; cast (hypereutectic) pistons, forged rods | in the car |
| 1.4T bored 1 mm with forged pistons | 73.5 x 82.6 | 1,402 cc, 86 ci | About the most the block takes; ZZP 1.4L forged long block (4340 rods, Diamond forged pistons, torque-plate hone) | $6,899.98 listed (ZZP), core required |
| 1.8 (2H0, from the Cruze LS) | 80.5 x 88.2 | 1,796 cc, 110 ci | Bolts into the car but it is not a turbo engine: no turbo oil feed, high-compression cast pistons, different ECU and harness | used engines are cheap; turbo conversion is all custom |
| 1.4T LE2 (2016+ Cruze) | 74 x 81.3 | 1,399 cc, 85 ci | Aluminum block; different electronics; no gain in size | not worth it |
| 2.0T LTG | 86 x 86 | 1,998 cc, 122 ci | No kits for a 1st-gen Cruze; custom mounts, wiring, ECU | well over $8,000 (estimate) |

**Biggest in its own block:** 1,402 cc (86 ci). **Biggest that bolts in:** the 1.8 (110 ci),
but it makes less power than a tuned 1.4T unless he builds a custom turbo system for it.

**Recommendation: keep the 1.4T at 1,364 cc.** No bigger engine is practical in this car: the
bigger options are either not turbo engines or need custom wiring and mounts that cost more than
the whole power budget. Power comes from the Z04 turbo and the tune. If the engine fails the
tests or he wants to go past about 250 hp, the move is a forged 1.4 (ZZP long block or the same
parts from a local machine shop), not a bigger engine.

## 2007 Suzuki Hayabusa (Gen 1)

**The build:** fix the oil drips first, MOSFET regulator and stator upgrade, 300 fat tire
stretch kit (C&S Customs or DSC), lowered, chrome (wheels, swingarm, trim), candy red to blue
fade with smoky diamonds and gold, gold calipers, LED underglow, custom diamond emblem, diamond
seat, speakers in the undertail and front inner fairing with an amp and Bluetooth.

**Total time:** about 4-8 months of weekends, mostly chrome and paint lead times.

### Step by step

**1. Baseline (1 weekend)**
- Do: finish title and registration before the temp tag expires. Record VIN and mileage.
  Compression test (compare to the service manual; cylinders within 10%) and leak-down. Charging
  test: stator AC output (about 60-70 V per pair at 5,000 rpm) and 13.8-14.5 V at the battery.
  Tire date codes, chain and sprockets. Clean the engine and find the drip source (Busa file
  list).
- Checkpoint: written list of what leaks and what is worn.

**2. Mechanical (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: countershaft seal, clutch slave, oil cooler O-rings (whatever the hunt found), full
  service, MOSFET regulator (Shindengen FH020AA with the Eastern Beaver adapter), battery,
  braided brake lines and pads.
- Have on hand: seals, O-rings, slave kit, fluids, regulator and adapter, brake lines.
- Checkpoint: 200 miles and a dry piece of cardboard under the bike overnight.

**3. Pick the stretch and order the kit (1 weekend of calls)**
- Do: decide the stretch (6 inches suggested for street). Call C&S Customs and DSC to confirm a
  Gen 1 300 kit is in stock. Order raw (unplated) so it can be test-fit first.

**4. Stretch, 300 tire and lowering test-fit (2-3 weekends)**
- Do: install the swingarm, wheel, 300 tire, offset front sprocket, chain, brake line
  extension. Lowering links and fork drop, matched front and rear. Ride it gently to check.
- Have on hand: rear stand, front stand, laser or string alignment tool, torque wrench.
- **Checkpoints:** chain to tire clearance at full suspension compression; chain line straight
  (laser); front and rear wheels in line (string line); swingarm pivot and axle torque; kickstand
  lean at the final height (shorten it now).
- Mistakes: sending parts to chrome before this fit. Any grinding or spacer change after plating
  ruins the chrome.

**5. Chrome (shop, 2-6 weeks)**
- Do: take the swingarm and wheels off and send them out for chrome (or order the chromed
  versions now that the fit is proven). Order chrome levers, mirrors, bar ends, pegs and bolts.

**6. Body prep and pods before paint (2-4 weekends)**
- Do: fit the new fairing kit on the bike (aftermarket kits often need holes and tabs adjusted).
  Build the speaker pods in the inner fairing and the undertail enclosure now, in fiberglass or
  3D print, so they get painted with the bike. Mark the underglow mounts and emblem locations.
  Plan the audio, amp and underglow wiring routes and the second battery spot.
- Mistakes: building pods after paint (means sanding and repainting new paint).
- Checkpoint: every panel and pod fits with the bike assembled; then strip the panels for paint.

**7. Paint (shop, 4-8 weeks)**
- Do: candy red to blue fade, smoky diamonds, gold pinstripes and leaf, diamond emblems; gold
  calipers.
- Mistakes: picking a painter without candy and gold leaf motorcycle work in their portfolio.

**8. Charging and electrical (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: higher-output stator (Ricks), second battery and isolator if the amp is over about 100 W
  RMS, then the amp, speakers, Bluetooth and underglow with sealed connectors and fuses.
- Checkpoint: at idle with the audio playing and lights on, battery voltage stays above about
  13.2 V.

**9. Final assembly, seat and tune (1-2 weekends)**
- Do: chrome parts on, painted panels on (blankets and tape on the paint), seat. If the intake
  or exhaust changed, a dyno tune (Power Commander or a Gen 1 ECU flash).
- Break-in: scrub the 300 tire for the first 100 miles; it steers heavier and wants to stand
  the bike up in turns.

### Don't do this before that (Hayabusa)

- Don't ride far before the drip is found and fixed.
- Don't add audio before the regulator and stator are upgraded.
- Don't chrome the swingarm or wheels before the stretch kit is test-fit.
- Don't paint before the fairing kit, speaker pods, emblem and underglow mounts are fitted.
- Don't shorten the kickstand before the final lowered height is set.

### Biggest engine for the Hayabusa (Gen 1, 1,299 cc)

The Gen 1 cylinders are plated aluminum, so any overbore means boring plus re-plating, or
sleeves for the larger sizes. Size = bore x bore x stroke x 0.7854 x 4.

| Build | Bore x stroke (mm) | Size | Notes | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stock** | 81 x 63 | 1,299 cc, 79 ci | | in the bike |
| 82 mm overbore | 82 x 63 | 1,331 cc, 81 ci | Small gain | similar cost to 84 mm |
| **84 mm big bore** | 84 x 63 | 1,397 cc, 85 ci | Most common street big bore; Wiseco CK181 84 mm kit (13.5:1 compression; pump gas per Orient Express) | pistons about $580-820 (Wiseco, JE listings); bore and re-plate extra; Orient Express sells a done 1407 block for $1,299 on exchange (listed for Gen 2; ask about Gen 1) |
| 86 mm sleeved | 86 x 63 | 1,464 cc, 89 ci | Needs sleeves; more heat and vibration | $2,000-3,500 (estimate) |
| Stroker | 84 x 67 (+4 mm crank) | 1,485 cc, 91 ci | Welded or billet crank, longer cylinders or spacer plate; drag builds go past 1,600 cc | $4,000-8,000+ (estimate) |

**Biggest:** 1,600 cc and up exists in drag racing (sleeves plus stroker). **Biggest that stays
streetable:** 1,397 cc.

**Recommendation: keep the stock 1,299 cc.** A Gen 1 Busa already makes about 150-160 hp at the
wheels; a stretched, lowered show bike on a 300 tire cannot use much more, and the stock engine
is the most reliable. If the leak-down test says the top end needs work anyway, that is the time
to do the 84 mm 1,397 cc kit (bore, re-plate, Wiseco or JE pistons, Cometic gasket) with a dyno
tune: modest gain, still pump gas and streetable. Skip sleeved and stroker builds for this bike.

## Top "do this first" items

1. **Charger:** oil change now, then compression and leak-down before buying the supercharger.

3. **Cutlass:** the 10-minute flashlight check for frame and floor rot, then brace the door
   openings before the body comes off.
