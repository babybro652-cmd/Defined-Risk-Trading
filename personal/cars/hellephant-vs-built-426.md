# Hellephant vs the built 426 (2016 Charger Pursuit, 11/2/2026)

Tim saw a 1969 Charger show car with a Mopar Hellephant (426 supercharged crate, about 1,000 hp,
950 lb-ft) and asked what the Hellephant route would cost in the Pursuit (RWD, 5.7, 8HP70 now).

**This is an option for Tim to decide. The chosen plan does not change:** forged 426 stroker,
Whipple 3.8L, E85 flex fuel, built 8HP90, about 800 whp, Charger total about $62,650–113,780
([build-plan.md](build-plan.md)).

Prices marked "checked 11/3" came from a listing that day. Everything else is an estimate:
confirm before buying. Sources at the end.

**Recommendation: keep the current plan.** The A30 costs $4,400–12,600 more for the same street
result (about 800-870 whp), and neither Hellephant is made for a 2011+ Charger, so the wiring and
computer side is custom either way. If Tim wants a factory-built, dyno-tested engine instead of a
shop-built one, the **C170** is the one worth a quote: about the same money as the plan, E85 like
the plan, and it is the Demon 170 engine from the same platform family as the Charger. Skip the A30.

## Fitment in a 2016 Charger (LD): what's needed

The Hellephant is a Gen III Hemi, the same engine family as the 5.7, 6.4 and Hellcat. Charger
Hellcats were built on this same LD body from 2015, so the engine fits the bay, the K-member and
the mounts. The problems are the parts around it.

| Item | Issue | Fix | Cost (est) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAN bus and body computers | The kit PCM is made for cars with no factory network. The Charger's body computer, key (RF hub, immobilizer), ABS and stability, cluster and the 8HP TCM all expect a factory engine computer on the CAN bus. With the kit PCM alone: no start or warning lights, no stability control, and the trans has no torque data | Skip the kit. Use a 2015+ Hellcat PCM and engine harness (Demon 170 parts for the C170), unlocked and custom tuned for the Hellephant, then program the body computer, RF hub and cluster to it (witech, VIN). This is how Hemi swap shops do modern Chargers | $3,000–6,500 (used PCM and harness, programming, custom tune including the TCM) |
| Hood | The 3.0L IHI blower is taller than the Hellcat's 2.4L | Cowl, Hellcat or Demon style vented hood; measure first | $800–2,000 |
| Accessory drive | Alternator, power steering and A/C are not on the engine | Hellcat accessory parts or the Direct Connection accessory kit, plus an A/C bracket | $800–2,000 |
| Flexplate | Comes with a flywheel (for a manual) | Hellcat automatic flexplate and crank adapter for the 8HP | $300–600 |
| Mounts, headers | 5.7 parts don't all carry over | Mounts, headers or 6.4 midpipes, gaskets, sensors | $800–1,800 |
| Cooling | Blower intercooler loop needs a heat exchanger and pump; radiator and oil/trans coolers for 1,000 hp | Same as the plan's cooling line | $1,500–3,000 |
| Fuel | 1,000 hp needs far more pump than the stock Charger module | A30: return-style dual pump, AN lines (injectors come on the engine). C170: the full E85 flex fuel line from the plan | A30 $1,500–3,000; C170 $2,500–4,500 |
| Transmission | 950 lb-ft is past a stock 8HP90 (about 664-738 lb-ft) | The plan's built 8HP90 (about 1,100 lb-ft) handles it | $9,000–15,000 (same line) |
| Diff, axles, driveshaft | Demon-spec diff, 1,400 hp axles and 1,000+ hp driveshaft are already in the plan | No change | same lines |
| Emissions | Direct Connection lists it for pre-1976 or track-only cars | Same issue as the built 426 with a blower. Person County has no emissions test; NC safety inspection still applies | n/a |

## Pros and cons

**Hellephant (A30 or C170)**

Pros:
- Built and dyno-tested by Mopar as one engine. Forged parts, blower, injectors and coils matched
  from the factory. Fewer parts to source and no machine shop.
- A30 makes the target power on pump gas. No E85 needed.
- Show-car look and the Hellephant name.

Cons:
- A30 costs $4,400–12,600 more than the plan.
- Made for pre-1976 cars. In a 2016 Charger the kit's PCM does not talk to the body computers,
  so the wiring and tune are custom work anyway, the same as the plan.
- 950 lb-ft at full tune is more than the 800 whp target needs: harder on the trans, diff and tires.
- Special order, VIN required, non-returnable; ask Direct Connection about warranty terms.
- C170: confirm it is still sold (the 11/2025 relaunch news covers the A30 and 354 Drag Pak only).

**Current plan (built 426 + Whipple 3.8L)**

Pros:
- Cheapest of the three at the low end. Sized to the target from day one.
- Street map about 650-700 whp on 93, race map about 800 whp on E85: easy on parts day to day.
- Whipple and the Hemi shop handle fit and tune.

Cons:
- Whipple 3.8L is a custom order for the 426 (fallback: 3.0L at higher boost).
- Shop-built engine: quality depends on the builder. Get two builder quotes.
